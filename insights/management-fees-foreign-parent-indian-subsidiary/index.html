Charging your Indian subsidiary: management fees that survive scrutiny

Group companies often recharge their Indian subsidiary for management, IT, HR, finance or brand support. It is a legitimate way to share costs and move money to the parent, but Indian tax officers test these charges closely. Here is how they are taxed, what documents protect the deduction, and where companies get caught.

Key takeaways
  • Withhold 20% or the treaty rate; US/UK/Singapore: make-available test.
  • Document benefit, cost base and mark-up for transfer pricing.
  • Pay 18% GST under reverse charge; credit usually available.
Quick answer: the Indian subsidiary can pay group service fees abroad without RBI approval. It must withhold tax first: 20% plus surcharge and cess for fees for technical services, or the treaty rate. Under the US, UK and Singapore treaties, many routine services are not taxable in India at all unless they “make available” know-how to the subsidiary. The subsidiary also pays 18% GST under reverse charge (usually recoverable), and must prove under transfer pricing that it actually received a benefit at an arm’s-length price.

The “make available” test

Under India’s treaties with the US, UK and Singapore (among others), a fee for technical or consultancy services is taxable in India only if the service makes available technical knowledge, skill, know-how or processes, so that the Indian company can apply it on its own in future. Routine support, such as running group payroll systems, regional management oversight or shared accounting, often doesn’t meet that test. Then the fee is business profits of the parent and is taxed in India only if the parent has a permanent establishment here. Treaties without this clause (many European and Asian treaties) tax such fees at the treaty rate, commonly 10%.

Where companies get caught

  • Shareholder activities charged as services: group board meetings, investor relations or the parent’s own reporting benefit the parent, not the subsidiary.
  • Duplicate services that the Indian team already performs.
  • Round-sum charges, such as a fixed percentage of sales, with no cost base or evidence.
  • No tax withheld because the parent “is not taxable in India”: the deduction is then disallowed unless the treaty position is documented.
  • GST missed on related-party services, which creates interest and penalties even though the credit would have been available.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Indian subsidiary pay management fees to its foreign parent?
Yes. Payments for genuine services are current account transactions and can be remitted through the bank without RBI approval or a cap, after withholding Indian tax and filing Forms 145 and 146 where required.
What is the TDS rate on management fees paid to a foreign company?
Under domestic law, fees for technical or managerial services paid to a non-resident are taxed at 20% plus surcharge and cess. A lower treaty rate applies with a tax residency certificate and Form 41, and under treaties with a make-available clause, routine services may not be taxable in India at all.
What does make available mean in a tax treaty?
It means the service transfers technical knowledge, skill, know-how or processes so the recipient can apply them independently in future. Under the India-US, India-UK and India-Singapore treaties, technical fees are taxable in India only if this test is met.
Is GST payable on services from a foreign parent to its Indian subsidiary?
Yes. The Indian subsidiary pays 18% GST under the reverse charge mechanism on imported services, and between related parties a supply can arise even without consideration. The subsidiary can usually claim input tax credit.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
