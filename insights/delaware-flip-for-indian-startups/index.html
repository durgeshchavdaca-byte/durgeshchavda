Delaware flip for Indian startups: should you set up a US holdco?

A Delaware parent can open doors to US capital and customers. It can also create tax and FEMA problems that take years to unwind. Here’s how to decide.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·9 min read
Discuss your structure hello@durgeshchavda.com

What is a Delaware flip?

A flip moves an Indian startup under a US parent company, usually a Delaware C-Corp. The founders and existing investors end up holding shares in the US company, and the Indian company becomes its subsidiary — typically where the team, operations and costs remain.

Why founders flip

  • Investor preference — many US funds and accelerators invest only in Delaware companies with standard documents.
  • US customers and credibility — contracting with enterprise customers as a US entity.
  • US hiring — stock options that US employees understand.
  • Exit path — US acquirers and listing options.

If most of your capital, customers and future exit are in India, those advantages shrink — and the costs remain.

How a flip usually works

  1. Incorporate the Delaware parent with a registered agent, and file the founders’ 83(b) elections where relevant (within 30 days of the stock grant).
  2. Value the Indian company — the valuation drives the swap ratio, FEMA pricing and capital-gains computation.
  3. Share swap — shareholders transfer their Indian shares to the US parent in exchange for US shares (or the parent is capitalised and acquires the Indian company).
  4. Regulatory filings — overseas investment filings by resident shareholders, and FEMA reporting of the non-resident parent’s holding in the Indian company (see FC-TRS / FC-GPR).
  5. Intercompany set-up — service or development agreements with transfer pricing, IP decisions and ESOP re-grants at the parent level (see US parent ESOPs for Indian employees).

FEMA and overseas investment (ODI)

Indian residents acquiring shares of the US parent are making an overseas investment, governed by the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Rules, 2022 and the related regulations and RBI directions. Two areas deserve particular care:

  • Resident individuals and control — the rules restrict certain investments by resident individuals in a foreign entity that itself has subsidiaries or step-down subsidiaries they control, which directly affects the classic “founders own the US parent, which owns India” structure.
  • Round-tripping and layers — investment that loops back into India through foreign layers is scrutinised, and limits on the number of subsidiary layers apply to certain structures.

This is why the sequence — who invests, from where, and in what form — has to be designed before anything is signed.

Tax considerations

  • Capital gains on the swap — for resident shareholders, exchanging Indian shares for foreign shares is generally a taxable transfer, with the gain driven by the valuation and holding period.
  • POEM — if key management decisions of the US company are effectively taken in India, it risks being treated as tax-resident in India under the Place of Effective Management rules.
  • Transfer pricing — the Indian subsidiary must be remunerated at arm’s length (commonly cost-plus) with documentation to match.
  • US tax and compliance — federal corporate tax, Delaware franchise tax and annual report, and US reporting for foreign-owned or foreign-subsidiary structures.
  • Withholding and DTAA — dividends, royalties and service fees between India and the US, and treaty relief.

The reverse-flip trend

In recent years several well-known Indian startups have moved their holding companies back to India ahead of an Indian listing — often paying significant tax to do so. India has also eased the mechanics of inbound share swaps. The lesson: a flip is easy to start and expensive to reverse, so it should follow a clear strategic reason.

A simple decision test

Lean towards a US holdco if…Lean towards staying Indian if…
Your lead investors require a Delaware entityMost of your investors are India-focused funds
Most revenue comes from US customersCustomers and revenue are primarily in India
You expect a US acquisition or listingAn Indian IPO is a realistic outcome
Founders are, or will be, based in the USFounders and management will stay in India

Frequently asked questions

Is a Delaware flip still worth it for Indian startups?
It depends on your investors, customers, founder location and exit path. It is most useful when US investors require it and the business is US-facing; for India-focused companies an Indian parent is often simpler and avoids reverse-flip costs later.
Is the share swap in a Delaware flip taxable in India?
Generally yes for resident shareholders — the exchange is treated as a transfer and can create capital-gains tax, based on valuation and holding period.
What is POEM and why does it matter for a flip?
POEM (Place of Effective Management) tests where key management and commercial decisions are actually made. If a US parent is effectively run from India, it may be treated as an Indian tax resident.
Which FEMA rules apply to a Delaware flip?
Resident shareholders acquiring the US parent's shares fall under the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Rules, 2022, while the US parent's holding in the Indian company is foreign investment reported under FEMA non-debt instrument rules (e.g., FC-TRS / FC-GPR).

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
