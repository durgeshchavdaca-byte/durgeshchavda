DPIIT startup recognition in 2026: the new rules

The government replaced the 2019 startup definition in February 2026. Limits went up, deep-tech startups got their own category, and cooperatives can now apply.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·7 min read
Get recognised hello@durgeshchavda.com

Who qualifies (notification of 4 February 2026)

StartupDeep-tech startup
AgeUp to 10 years from incorporation or registrationUp to 20 years
Turnover limit (any year)₹200 crore (was ₹100 crore)₹300 crore
Other testsWorking on innovation or a scalable model with high job or wealth creation potentialSolutions based on new scientific or engineering knowledge, high R&D spend, significant novel IP
  • Eligible entities: private limited companies, LLPs, registered partnership firms and — new in 2026 — cooperative societies.
  • The business shouldn’t be formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business, and the new rules restrict diverting funds into real estate, luxury assets or securities.
  • Already recognised? You don’t need to reapply.

Benefits

  • IP support: fee rebates on patents and trademarks and expedited patent examination.
  • Compliance relief: self-certification under specified labour and environmental laws (check how this now maps to the new Labour Codes).
  • Funding: access to the Fund of Funds for Startups, and the new ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds 2.0 focused on deep tech and manufacturing.
  • Tax: eligibility for the startup tax holiday and the ESOP tax deferral for employees (see below).

The startup tax holiday

  • A 100% deduction of profits for any 3 consecutive years out of the first 10 — section 140 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly 80-IAC).
  • Requires DPIIT recognition plus a certificate from the Inter-Ministerial Board.
  • The company must be incorporated before 1 April 2030, and the Finance Act 2026 raised this section’s turnover limit to ₹300 crore.
  • The same certificate lets employees defer tax on ESOPs — see ESOP taxation.

How to apply

  1. Register the entity on the Startup India portal.
  2. Fill the recognition form with incorporation details, a description of the innovation and supporting documents (pitch deck, website, patents or awards if any).
  3. Track the application and respond to queries.
  4. For the tax holiday, apply separately to the Inter-Ministerial Board after recognition.

Raising money after recognition? See fundraising advisory and angel tax.

Frequently asked questions

What is the turnover limit for DPIIT startup recognition in 2026?
₹200 crore in any financial year for a startup, and ₹300 crore for the new deep-tech startup category, under the notification of 4 February 2026.
How old can a company be to get DPIIT recognition?
Up to 10 years from incorporation or registration, or up to 20 years for a deep-tech startup.
Does DPIIT recognition give a tax holiday?
It's the first step. The tax holiday — 100% of profits for 3 consecutive years out of 10 under section 140 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 — also needs an Inter-Ministerial Board certificate, and the company must be incorporated before 1 April 2030.
Can an LLP get DPIIT recognition?
Yes. Private limited companies, LLPs, registered partnership firms and, since 2026, cooperative societies can be recognised.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
