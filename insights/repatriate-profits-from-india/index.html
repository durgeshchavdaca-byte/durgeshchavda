Foreign parent guide

Getting profits out of your Indian subsidiary

“How do we get profit out of our India subsidiary, and what is withheld?” is one of the most common questions from foreign CFOs. Here are the routes, the tax on each and the paperwork.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 14 October 2026·6 min read
Plan your repatriation hello@durgeshchavda.com
Key takeaways
  • Dividends to a foreign company: 20% plus surcharge and cess, or the lower treaty rate with a TRC and Form 41.
  • Service fees and royalties are deductible in India if at arm's length, but carry withholding.
  • From 1 April 2026, buybacks are taxed as capital gains rather than dividends.

The five routes

RouteDeductible for the Indian company?Indian withholding (domestic)
DividendNo — paid from post-tax profit20% plus surcharge and cess

Royalty / licence feeYes, if at arm’s length20%; usually 10–15% under treaties

Buyback of sharesNoCapital gains in the parent’s hands from 1 April 2026

Dividend withholding by country

Parent company inTreaty rate on dividends

Treaty rates need a tax residency certificate, Form 41 and beneficial ownership of the dividend; anti-abuse rules (the MLI principal purpose test) can deny them. Check the treaty and its protocols for your case.

The paperwork for every remittance

  • From the parent: a tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly 10F) and, for fees and royalties, a no-PE declaration.
  • From the Indian company: withholding under section 393(2), Form 145 online and a CA certificate in Form 146 where required, then the bank remittance.
  • Board approvals for dividends; intercompany agreements and transfer-pricing support for fees, royalties and interest; ECB rules for loans.

Choosing the mix

  • Dividends are simple but come from post-tax profit; fees and royalties reduce Indian tax but invite transfer-pricing scrutiny.
  • Withholding on fees can often be credited in the parent’s country — check your foreign tax credit position.
  • Since April 2026, buybacks are taxed as capital gains (on the gain, not the whole payout) — worth modelling against a dividend.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax is withheld on dividends paid to a foreign parent from India?
20% plus surcharge and cess under domestic law, reduced by the treaty — for example 15% for a US parent holding at least 10%, and 10% for UK, UAE, Japanese, German or Dutch parents — with a tax residency certificate and Form 41.
Can an Indian subsidiary pay management fees to its foreign parent?
Yes, if the services are real, priced at arm's length and documented. Withholding applies under section 393(2), often at a reduced treaty rate, and the fees are deductible for the Indian company.
How are share buybacks taxed in India from 2026?
From 1 April 2026 buyback proceeds are taxed as capital gains in the shareholder's hands — on the gain, after deducting cost — rather than as dividends. Promoters face additional tax.
Is there a limit on repatriating profits from India?
There is no cap on repatriating dividends from profits on which tax has been paid, provided company-law requirements are met and the remittance is documented with the bank.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
