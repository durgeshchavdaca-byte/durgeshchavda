Foreign vendor guide

Form 41, TRC and no-PE declaration — for foreign vendors paid by India

Indian customers withhold tax from many payments to foreign suppliers. These three documents are how you get your treaty rate instead of 20% — here’s what each one is and how to get it right.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 30 October 2026·6 min read
Get your vendor pack ready hello@durgeshchavda.com
Key takeaways
  • Form 10F was renumbered Form 41 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026.
  • A TRC, Form 41 and a no-PE declaration let an Indian payer apply your treaty rate instead of 20%.
  • If tax was withheld at a lower treaty rate, a foreign company usually still has to file an Indian return.

What is Form 41 in India?

Form 41 is the form a non-resident files on India’s income-tax portal to claim tax-treaty benefits. It replaced Form 10F from 1 April 2026 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026, and gives the details a tax residency certificate may not contain — status, nationality, tax identification number, period and address. Indian payers ask for it, with a TRC and often a no-PE declaration, before applying a treaty withholding rate to payments abroad.

Why your Indian customer is asking

Indian payers must withhold tax on many payments to non-residents under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 — royalties and fees for technical services at 20% plus surcharge and cess under domestic law. To apply the lower rate in your country’s tax treaty (often 10–15%, or nil for ordinary business profits without a PE), they need evidence. If they get it wrong, the shortfall is theirs — so they ask.

Form 41: the new name for Form 10F (what the official FAQ says)

  • From 1 April 2026, Form 10F (section 90(5) of the 1961 Act, Rule 21AB) became Form 41, a self-declaration under section 159(8) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 and Rule 75 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Indian payers may still say “10F”.
  • Mandatory for a non-resident to claim treaty benefits. Without a valid Form 41 (with the TRC), the Indian payer must deduct tax at the full domestic rate, and an exemption claimed in a return is taxed at Indian rates.
  • Online only, on the income-tax e-filing portal. No PAN needed: non-residents without a PAN register under the new “non-residents without PAN” category and verify by OTP sent to phone and email. With a PAN, verify by EVC (bank or demat account), net banking or a digital signature.
  • Once per tax year (April to March). There is no fixed deadline, but it must be in place when treaty benefits are claimed, so file it before the first payment of the year.
  • Attach: your tax residency certificate, valid for the relevant Indian financial year, and your tax identification number from your home country.
  • Cannot be edited once submitted and acknowledged, so check every detail first. No proof of tax payment is needed, and Aadhaar is not required.

Is the no-PE declaration true for you?

Only sign it if it’s accurate. Staff or contractors in India who conclude contracts for you, an office at your disposal, or long on-site projects can create a PE — see PE risk.

Sample no-PE declaration (format)

Many Indian payers have their own template; if not, a declaration on the vendor’s letterhead usually covers these points. Illustrative only — adapt it to your facts and the relevant tax treaty, and check it with your adviser.

Declaration regarding permanent establishment

To: [Name of Indian payer], [address]
Date: [date]

We, [legal name of the foreign company], a company incorporated in [country] with its registered office at [address], declare for the period [from] to [to] that:

  1. We are a tax resident of [country] for this period, as shown in the enclosed Tax Residency Certificate issued by [authority].
  2. We do not have a permanent establishment in India within the meaning of Article 5 of the India–[country] Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement — including a fixed place of business, a dependent agent, or a service or installation PE.
  3. The income received from you under [invoice / agreement reference] is not effectively connected with any permanent establishment or fixed base in India.
  4. We are the beneficial owner of this income.
  5. We will inform you promptly if any of these facts change during the period.

Signed for and on behalf of [company] — [name], [designation]

Send it with the TRC and Form 41 (the information the TRC does not show). Only sign it if it is true — see is the no-PE declaration true for you?

After you’re paid

  • Your customer issues a certificate of the tax withheld; claim it as a foreign tax credit at home.
  • If too much was withheld, a refund is claimed through an Indian return.
  • If your only Indian income is royalties or technical fees and tax was withheld at the full domestic rate, you may not need to file. If a lower treaty rate was used, you usually do.
  • Large recurring contracts: a lower-withholding certificate (Form 128) can help.

Frequently asked questions

Is Form 41 the same as Form 10F?
Yes. Form 10F under the Income-tax Rules, 1962 became Form 41 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026, from 1 April 2026.
What is Form 41 in India?
Form 41 is the Income-tax Rules 2026 replacement for Form 10F. A non-resident files it online to give the details a tax residency certificate may not contain, so that an Indian payer can apply treaty withholding rates.
What is a no PE declaration?
A declaration by a foreign company that it has no permanent establishment in India. Indian payers use it, with a TRC and Form 41, to apply treaty rates — for business profits, usually nil withholding.
Is a PAN mandatory for a foreign company receiving payments from India?
Not always. Without a PAN the payer may have to withhold at a higher rate unless prescribed details are provided, and filing an Indian return needs one. Form 41 itself can be filed without a PAN.
Can Form 41 be filed without a PAN?
Yes. According to the Income Tax Department's Form 41 FAQs, non-residents without a PAN can register on the e-filing portal under the category for non-residents without PAN and file Form 41 online, verifying by OTP.
Is Form 41 mandatory, and how often is it filed?
Yes, it is mandatory to claim treaty benefits. It is filed online once per tax year, with a tax residency certificate valid for that year and your home-country tax identification number.
Can I edit Form 41 after submitting it?
No. Once Form 41 is submitted and the acknowledgement is generated, it cannot be edited, so check the details before you submit.
How long is a TRC valid for Indian treaty purposes?
It covers the period stated on it — usually a tax year. Provide a TRC and Form 41 covering each period in which you receive payments.
What is the format of a no-PE declaration in India?
A short letter on the foreign company's letterhead stating its tax residence (with the TRC), that it has no permanent establishment in India under Article 5 of the relevant tax treaty, that the income is not connected with any PE in India, that it is the beneficial owner, and that it will report changes — signed by an authorised person.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
