NRI in Singapore? Singapore rarely taxes your Indian income, so India’s tax is the cost

Singapore taxes individuals on income earned in Singapore. Overseas income that an individual receives in Singapore, such as Indian rent, interest and dividends, is generally not taxable there, and Singapore has no capital gains tax. For an Indian living in Singapore, the Indian tax on Indian income is therefore usually the only tax, and the India–Singapore treaty is the way to reduce it.

Key takeaways
  • India taxes only your Indian income while you are non-resident.
  • A tax residency certificate and Form 41 unlock treaty rates on interest and dividends.
  • File an Indian return to recover excess TDS; 31 December 2026 for FY 2025-26.
Quick answer: India taxes your Indian income: rent, NRO interest, dividends and gains on Indian assets. Singapore generally does not tax overseas income received by individuals. With a Singapore certificate of residence, the treaty caps Indian tax on NRO interest and dividends at 15%. NRE and FCNR interest is tax-free in India while you are an NRI. File an Indian return to get back excess TDS.

Residency: get this right first

  • Singapore certificate: the tax authority issues a certificate of residence to individuals who are tax resident, broadly those who live or work in Singapore for 183 days or more in a year.
  • Days in India: stay under 182 days, or under 120 days if your Indian income is above ₹15 lakh, to remain non-resident in India.
  • Singapore company owners: if you control a Singapore company from India during long stays, its place of management and your own residency both need checking.

The paperwork that saves tax

  1. Tax residency certificate from Singapore for each year, plus Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) filed on the Indian portal, to claim treaty rates.
  2. NRO, NRE and FCNR status updated with every Indian bank, broker and fund house.
  3. Lower-TDS certificate (Form 128) before a property sale or large payment, so tax is deducted on the real gain.
  4. Indian return for any year with TDS or taxable income. For FY 2025-26, the last date is 31 December 2026.
  5. Repatriation: up to USD 1 million a year from the NRO account, with Forms 15CA and 15CB.

If you move back to India

If you move back to India, the first two or three years are usually “not ordinarily resident”, and Singapore income and savings stay outside Indian tax. After that, worldwide income is taxable and Singapore bank accounts, CPF, SRS and company shares must be reported in India every year.

Held accounts or assets abroad in a year you were resident in India? Check whether FAST-DS applies before 31 December 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Do NRIs in Singapore pay tax in India?
Yes, on Indian income such as rent, NRO interest, dividends and capital gains on Indian assets. Singapore salary is not taxed in India while the person is non-resident.
Does Singapore tax my Indian income?
Generally no. Overseas income received in Singapore by an individual, including money deposited into a Singapore bank account, is generally not taxable, and Singapore has no capital gains tax.
What is the TDS rate on NRO interest for Singapore residents?
About 30% by default. With a Singapore certificate of residence and Form 41, the India-Singapore treaty rate of 15% can apply.
Do I need to report CPF in India after moving back?
Yes, once you are resident and ordinarily resident in India, CPF and other Singapore accounts are foreign assets to report every year. If earlier years were missed, FAST-DS can regularise them before 31 December 2026.
