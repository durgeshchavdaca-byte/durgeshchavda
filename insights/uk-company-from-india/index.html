Registering a UK company from India: what changed in 2025–26

You don’t need to live in the UK to own or direct a UK company. But Companies House now verifies every director’s identity, and fees went up in 2026.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 6 October 2026·6 min read
Plan your UK company hello@durgeshchavda.com

UK rules

  • Entity: private company limited by shares, registered at Companies House.
  • Fee: £100 for digital incorporation (£124 on paper) from 1 February 2026; the annual confirmation statement costs £50.
  • Identity verification: since 18 November 2025, directors must verify their identity before incorporation, and people with significant control within 14 days. Non-UK residents can verify through GOV.UK One Login with a biometric passport, or through an authorised corporate service provider.
  • Registered office: must be in the UK. At least one director is needed; a company secretary is optional.

Tax

  • Corporation tax 19% on profits up to £50,000, 25% above £250,000, with marginal relief in between.
  • India–UK treaty rates and the 2026 trade deal: see our UK page.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Indian resident be a director of a UK company?
Yes. UK companies don't need a UK-resident director, but every director must verify their identity with Companies House, and the company needs a UK registered office.
How much does it cost to register a UK company?
The Companies House fee is £100 for digital incorporation from 1 February 2026 (£124 on paper), plus £50 a year for the confirmation statement.
What is the UK corporation tax rate?
19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% on profits above £250,000, with marginal relief in between.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
