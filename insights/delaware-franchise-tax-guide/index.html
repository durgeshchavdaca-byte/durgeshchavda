Delaware franchise tax: how a $85,000 bill becomes $450

Every year thousands of startups get a Delaware notice for tens of thousands of dollars. Most of them owe a few hundred. Here’s why — and how to file correctly.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·7 min read
Get your filing done hello@durgeshchavda.com

Who pays, and when

EntityWhat’s dueDeadline
Delaware corporationAnnual report ($50 filing fee) + franchise tax1 March
Delaware LLC / LPFlat annual tax of $400 (no annual report) — up from $300 under HB 4001 June

Corporations owing $5,000 or more pay in instalments during the year. The tax applies whether or not the company has revenue or does business in Delaware.

The two calculation methods

Authorized Shares method (the default the notice uses)

  • Up to 5,000 authorized shares: $175 minimum.
  • 5,001–10,000 shares: $250.
  • Each additional 10,000 shares (or part): +$85.

Startups typically authorize around 10 million shares — which under this method produces a bill of about $85,165.

Assumed Par Value Capital (APVC) method

This method uses your gross assets (from your balance sheet / Form 1120 Schedule L) and issued shares:

  1. Assumed par = total gross assets ÷ total issued shares.
  2. If assumed par is at least the stated par value, multiply it by authorized shares; otherwise use stated par × authorized shares. This is the assumed par value capital.
  3. Tax = $400 per $1,000,000 of assumed par value capital (rounded up to the next million above $1M; proportionate below it), with a $400 minimum.

Delaware lets you pay whichever method gives the lower tax — but you must file with the APVC figures to get it.

Worked example

InputValue
Authorized shares10,000,000
Issued shares8,000,000
Par value$0.00001
Gross assets (31 Dec)$500,000
  • Authorized Shares method: $250 + (999 × $85) = $85,165
  • APVC method: $500,000 ÷ 8,000,000 = $0.0625 assumed par → × 10,000,000 = $625,000 → below $1M, so the $400 minimum applies
  • Total due: $400 tax + $50 annual report fee = $450

How to file

  1. Close your books for the year and get total gross assets.
  2. Confirm issued and authorized shares from your cap table.
  3. File the annual report online on the Delaware Division of Corporations site, entering gross assets and issued shares so the APVC calculation applies.
  4. List directors and an officer, and pay the tax plus the $50 fee before 1 March.

Late filing and penalties

Missing the deadline adds a $200 penalty plus 1.5% interest per month on unpaid tax, and a company that keeps missing filings loses its good standing — which you will need for fundraising, banking and any exit.

What about LLCs?

Delaware LLCs don’t use either method: they pay a flat $400 by 1 June — Delaware raised this from $300 under House Bill 400, effective for the 2026 tax year. Foreign-owned LLCs also have a separate federal filing — see Form 5472.

Setting up a new company? Choose authorized shares and par value with this tax in mind — US company registration.

Frequently asked questions

When is Delaware franchise tax due?
Corporations must file the annual report and pay franchise tax by 1 March each year. LLCs and LPs pay a flat $400 annual tax by 1 June (up from $300 from the 2026 tax year).
Why is my Delaware franchise tax so high?
Delaware's notice calculates tax using the Authorized Shares method, which can produce very large amounts for startups with millions of authorized shares. Filing with the Assumed Par Value Capital method, using your gross assets and issued shares, usually reduces it dramatically.
What is the minimum Delaware franchise tax?
$175 under the Authorized Shares method and $400 under the Assumed Par Value Capital method, plus a $50 annual report fee for corporations.
What is the penalty for filing late?
A $200 late penalty plus interest of 1.5% per month on unpaid tax.
