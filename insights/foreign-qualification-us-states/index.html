Foreign qualification: when your Delaware company must register in another state

Delaware is where your company is formed — not necessarily where it does business. Hire someone in California or open a desk in Texas, and that state expects your company to register and pay its taxes too.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·7 min read
Check where you must register hello@durgeshchavda.com

What foreign qualification means

In US terms, a Delaware corporation is a “foreign” corporation in every other state. Before it transacts business in another state, it generally must register there — called foreign qualification — by filing an application with that state’s Secretary of State and appointing a registered agent in that state. It then files that state’s annual reports and taxes, in addition to Delaware’s.

What usually triggers it

  • Employees working in the state — including a single remote employee, in many states.
  • An office, warehouse or other physical presence.
  • Inventory or property located in the state.
  • Significant in-state activity such as regular in-person sales or services.

Merely having customers in a state, or holding board meetings there, often isn’t enough on its own — but tax “doing business” tests can be broader than the registration tests. Sales tax is a separate question again: see US sales tax for SaaS.

California, Texas and New York

StateKey points for a Delaware C-Corp
CaliforniaRegister with the Secretary of State. Corporations doing business in California owe at least the $800 minimum franchise tax each year (newly qualified corporations are generally exempt from the minimum in their first taxable year), and California’s “doing business” test can be met by in-state employees or by California sales above an annually indexed threshold.
TexasRegister with the Secretary of State. Texas franchise tax applies to entities doing business there, but for 2026 reports entities with annualised total revenue up to $2.65 million owe no tax and don’t file a franchise tax report — they still file a Public Information or Ownership Information Report.
New YorkFile an application for authority with the Department of State; New York corporate franchise tax and publication-type requirements for some entity types may follow.

Every state sets its own forms, fees and annual deadlines — these add rows to your compliance calendar.

How to register

  1. Get a certificate of good standing from Delaware — your Delaware annual report and franchise tax must be current.
  2. Appoint a registered agent in the new state (the same provider can often cover multiple states — see registered agents).
  3. File the state’s foreign registration (statement, application for authority or equivalent) and pay the fee.
  4. Register for state taxes — income or franchise tax, payroll withholding and unemployment insurance if you have employees there, and sales tax if applicable.
  5. Calendar the annual filings for that state.

If you don’t register

  • Back fees, taxes, penalties and interest for the period you operated without registering.
  • No access to that state’s courts — an unregistered company generally can’t sue there (for example, to collect an unpaid invoice) until it registers.
  • Problems in due diligence — investors and acquirers check state registrations against where your team actually works.

Hiring is the most common trigger for Indian-founded startups: see hiring your first US employee.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Delaware company need to register in California?
If it transacts intrastate business in California — for example, with employees or an office there — it generally must register with the California Secretary of State, and if it is doing business in California it owes at least the $800 minimum franchise tax each year.
Is there a first-year exemption from California's $800 minimum tax?
Newly incorporated or newly qualified corporations are generally exempt from the minimum franchise tax in their first taxable year; LLCs do not have that exemption.
Does a small company owe Texas franchise tax?
For 2026 reports, entities with annualised total revenue at or below $2.65 million owe no franchise tax and don't file a franchise tax report, but must still file a Public Information or Ownership Information Report.
What happens if a company does business in a state without registering?
States can impose back fees, taxes, penalties and interest, and an unregistered company generally cannot bring a lawsuit in that state's courts until it registers.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
