ESOPs from a US parent: what Indian employees and employers must know

Your Delaware parent wants to give stock options to the team in India. It’s allowed — but the Indian subsidiary has reporting duties, and employees face tax at two points.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·8 min read
Set up cross-border ESOPs hello@durgeshchavda.com

Is it allowed?

Yes. Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Rules, 2022, a resident individual can acquire shares of a foreign company under an ESOP (or similar employee benefit scheme) when they are an employee or director of that company’s Indian office, branch or subsidiary, or of an Indian entity in which the foreign company holds equity directly or indirectly. This is the standard path for a Delaware C-Corp with an Indian subsidiary.

FEMA: portfolio (OPI) or direct (ODI) investment?

  • Shares acquired under ESOPs are treated as Overseas Portfolio Investment (OPI) when they are below 10% of the foreign company’s equity and don’t give control — the usual case for employees.
  • A founder or senior executive receiving 10% or more, or control, falls into ODI territory, with its own conditions and reporting. See ODI rules for US companies.
  • Remittances to pay the exercise price go through the employee’s bank under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme.

Reporting by the Indian employer

Form OPI, twice a year. The Indian subsidiary (not the employee) reports ESOP issuances and exercises to its AD bank in Form OPI, within 60 days of the end of each half-year (September and March). Cashless exercises are reported too.

Employees generally don’t make a separate FEMA filing for shares acquired this way — but they do report them in their income-tax return (see Schedule FA below).

Tax for employees in India

EventIndian tax treatment
Grant and vesting of optionsGenerally no tax
ExerciseFair market value minus exercise price is a perquisite, taxed as salary at slab rates; the employer deducts TDS
RSU vestingFull fair market value at vesting is taxed as salary
Sale — held 24 months or lessShort-term capital gain at slab rates
Sale — held more than 24 monthsLong-term capital gain at 12.5%, without indexation

Shares of a foreign company — even one listed on NASDAQ or NYSE — are treated like unlisted shares for Indian holding-period purposes. The cost for capital gains is the fair market value that was taxed as a perquisite at exercise.

Employer obligations

  • Valuation: for unlisted shares, fair market value for the perquisite must be determined as the income-tax rules require (by a SEBI-registered merchant banker). A US 409A valuation doesn’t replace it.
  • TDS: the Indian employer withholds tax on the perquisite through payroll, even though the shares come from the US parent.
  • Recharge arrangements: if the US parent recharges the ESOP cost to the Indian subsidiary, it becomes an international transaction for transfer pricing and has FEMA remittance implications.
  • Plan documents: an India sub-plan or addendum is common, to align exercise mechanics, tax withholding and FEMA reporting with Indian rules.

Schedule FA: don’t forget it

Resident employees holding foreign shares must disclose them in Schedule FA of their income-tax return — including the value at the end of the calendar year — even if nothing was sold. Missing this can attract heavy penalties under the Black Money Act, so employers often remind staff at filing time.

Common pitfalls

  • Indian subsidiary not filing Form OPI because “the shares are issued by the US company”.
  • Using the US 409A value for Indian perquisite tax.
  • No TDS on exercise because payroll didn’t know the exercise happened.
  • Employees omitting vested or exercised shares from Schedule FA.
  • Cashless exercises or sale-to-cover transactions not reported.

Frequently asked questions

Can a US company give stock options to employees in India?
Yes. Under the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022, employees and directors of the foreign company's Indian office, branch or subsidiary (or an Indian entity in which it holds equity) can acquire its shares under an ESOP.
Who reports foreign ESOPs to the RBI?
The Indian employer reports in Form OPI through its AD bank, half-yearly, within 60 days of the end of March and September. Employees generally have no separate FEMA filing.
How are foreign ESOPs taxed in India?
The difference between fair market value and exercise price is taxed as a salary perquisite at exercise, with TDS by the employer. On sale, gains are short-term at slab rates if held up to 24 months and long-term at 12.5% if held longer.
Do I need to report US ESOP shares in my Indian tax return?
Yes. Resident taxpayers must disclose foreign shares in Schedule FA of their income-tax return, even if they have not sold them.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
