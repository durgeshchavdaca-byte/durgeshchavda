Foreign shareholder in an Indian private company? Your shares now need to be in demat form

Since 30 June 2025, most Indian private companies must issue shares only in electronic (demat) form, and a shareholder who wants to transfer shares or subscribe to new ones must first convert existing physical shares. Every Indian subsidiary of a foreign company is covered. For a foreign parent or JV partner, that means a demat account in India before your next capital infusion, share transfer or exit.

Key takeaways
  • Rule 9B: private companies (not small) demat-only since 30 June 2025.
  • Subsidiaries are never small companies, so foreign groups are covered.
  • Demat your holding before the next capital infusion or transfer.
Quick answer: under Rule 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, every private company other than a small company had to move to demat by 30 June 2025. A company that is a subsidiary or holding company is never a “small company”, so every Indian subsidiary of a foreign group is covered, with no exemption for wholly owned subsidiaries. The company must have an ISIN, new shares can only be issued in demat form, physical transfers cannot be registered, and a shareholder must dematerialise existing shares before transferring or subscribing. A foreign shareholder therefore needs a demat account with an Indian depository participant.

How a foreign company opens a demat account in India

  1. Choose a depository participant (DP): a bank or broker registered with NSDL or CDSL that onboards foreign entities.
  2. Get a PAN for the foreign company if it does not have one (it usually needs one anyway for Indian tax).
  3. KYC documents: certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution authorising the account and signatories, proof of address, and identity documents of the signatories and beneficial owners, usually notarised and apostilled.
  4. Account type: the DP sets up the account for a foreign direct investor (FDI holding), distinct from portfolio investment accounts.
  5. Dematerialise: the Indian company (with its registrar, RTA) confirms the ISIN; you submit the physical share certificates through the DP for conversion.

Expect a few weeks end to end, mainly for apostilled documents. Start before you need to infuse capital or sign a share transfer.

What the Indian company must do

  • Appoint a registrar and transfer agent (RTA) and obtain an ISIN for each class of securities.
  • Ensure promoters, directors and key managerial personnel hold their securities in demat before any new offer.
  • File the half-yearly reconciliation of share capital audit report (Form PAS-6) where applicable.
  • Pay depository fees and keep the register of members in line with depository records.

Frequently asked questions

Do foreign shareholders of an Indian private company need a demat account?
Usually yes. Since 30 June 2025, private companies other than small companies must issue securities only in demat form, and a holder must dematerialise existing shares before transferring them or subscribing to new ones. A foreign shareholder needs a demat account with an Indian depository participant.
Is an Indian wholly owned subsidiary of a foreign company exempt from demat rules?
No. A subsidiary or holding company cannot be a small company under the Companies Act, and Rule 9B has no exemption for wholly owned subsidiaries, so the Indian subsidiary and its foreign parent's holding are covered.
Can a foreign parent invest more in its Indian subsidiary if its shares are physical?
Not until it converts them. New shares can only be allotted in demat form, and the subscriber must first dematerialise its existing holding under Rule 9B.
What documents does a foreign company need to open a demat account in India?
Typically a PAN, certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, a board resolution, proof of address and identity documents of signatories and beneficial owners, usually notarised and apostilled. Requirements vary by depository participant.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
