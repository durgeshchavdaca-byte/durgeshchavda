Form 5472: the $25,000 form foreign-owned US LLCs forget

Your LLC made no money, so there’s nothing to file — right? Not if you live outside the US. Here’s what Form 5472 is and how to stay clear of the penalty.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 6 October 2026·7 min read
Get Form 5472 filed hello@durgeshchavda.com

Who must file Form 5472

  • Foreign-owned single-member LLCs (US “disregarded entities” owned by one non-US person) — every year they have a reportable transaction with the owner or a related party.
  • US corporations that are at least 25% foreign-owned and have reportable transactions with a foreign related party.

For an India-based founder, the most common case is a one-person LLC formed to invoice US clients or hold a US bank account.

What counts as a reportable transaction

  • Money you put in — including the initial capital to open the bank account.
  • Money you take out — distributions or withdrawals.
  • Loans between you and the company, and interest.
  • Payments for services, rent, royalties or goods between the company and you or your other companies.
  • Formation costs paid by the owner on the company’s behalf.

Because forming and funding the LLC is itself a transaction, almost every foreign-owned LLC has to file in its first year.

How to file

  1. The LLC needs an EIN.
  2. Prepare a pro forma Form 1120 — only the identifying information is filled in, marked as a foreign-owned disregarded entity filing.
  3. Attach Form 5472 reporting the owner and all reportable transactions for the year.
  4. Submit it to the IRS in the manner set out in the current Form 5472 instructions (these returns are generally faxed or mailed, not e-filed through standard software).

Deadline

15 April for calendar-year entities. A six-month extension is available by filing Form 7004 by the original due date.

Penalties

The penalty for failing to file, filing late or filing an incomplete Form 5472 is $25,000 per form, per year. If the failure continues more than 90 days after the IRS notifies you, a further $25,000 applies for each 30-day period. Penalties apply even when the LLC owes no tax.

Records to keep

Keep bank statements, invoices, agreements and a simple ledger of every transfer between you and the LLC. The rules require records that support the return, and they make next year’s filing quick.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to file Form 5472 if my LLC had no income?
Usually yes. If the foreign-owned LLC had any reportable transaction with its owner — including the money used to open its bank account — a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 is required even with zero income.
When is Form 5472 due?
With the pro forma Form 1120, by 15 April for calendar-year entities, extendable by six months with Form 7004 filed by the original due date.
What is the penalty for not filing Form 5472?
$25,000 per failure, with additional $25,000 penalties for each 30 days the failure continues beyond 90 days after IRS notice.
Does a US C-Corp owned by Indians file Form 5472?
A US corporation that is at least 25% foreign-owned files Form 5472 with its Form 1120 for reportable transactions with foreign related parties.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
