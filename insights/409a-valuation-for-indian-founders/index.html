409A valuation for Indian founders: one company, two valuations

Your Delaware company grants options to people in the US and in India. The US wants a 409A valuation when you grant; India wants a merchant-banker valuation when your team exercises. Here’s how to keep both clean.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·7 min read

What a 409A valuation is

US tax rules (Section 409A) treat stock options granted with an exercise price below fair market value as deferred compensation. For US taxpayers holding such options, the consequences are harsh: income inclusion as the options vest, plus a 20% additional tax and interest. So Delaware startups set the exercise price at no less than the fair market value of the common stock on the grant date — and support it with a 409A valuation.

  • A valuation by a qualified independent appraiser using accepted methods is presumed reasonable (a “safe harbour”), which shifts the burden to the IRS to challenge it.
  • It generally remains usable for 12 months, unless a material event happens first — a priced round, a major contract, an acquisition offer.

When you need one

  • Before your first option grants.
  • At least every 12 months while you keep granting.
  • After any material event, such as closing a priced round (converting SAFEs usually counts).

The Indian valuation for employees in India

For employees resident in India, what matters is Indian tax on exercise: the difference between fair market value on the exercise date and the exercise price is a salary perquisite, with TDS by the Indian employer. For shares of an unlisted company — which a private Delaware C-Corp is — the income-tax rules require that fair market value to be determined by a SEBI-registered Category I merchant banker, on a valuation date no more than 180 days before the exercise date.

A 409A report doesn’t satisfy this requirement. More on the Indian side: US parent ESOPs for Indian employees.

Side-by-side

409A valuation (US)Merchant-banker valuation (India)
PurposeSet a compliant exercise price at grantMeasure the taxable perquisite at exercise
Who performs itQualified independent appraiser (for safe harbour)SEBI-registered Category I merchant banker
How long it lastsUp to 12 months, absent a material eventValuation date within 180 days before exercise
Who relies on itThe US company and US-taxpayer option holdersThe Indian employer (for TDS) and India-resident employees

Timing in practice

  1. Refresh the 409A annually and after each priced round, before approving new grants.
  2. Batch exercise windows for India-based staff (for example, quarterly), so one merchant-banker valuation can cover several exercises within 180 days.
  3. Tell Indian payroll before each exercise — the Indian subsidiary deducts TDS on the perquisite even though the shares come from the US parent.
  4. Report the issuance in Form OPI half-yearly through the Indian subsidiary’s AD bank.

Common pitfalls

  • Granting options before any 409A report exists, or after a round without refreshing it.
  • Using the 409A value for Indian perquisite tax.
  • Exercises happening outside the 180-day window of the latest merchant-banker valuation.
  • Employees forgetting to report the shares in Schedule FA of their Indian return.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 409A valuation?
An independent valuation of a private US company's common stock used to set stock-option exercise prices at no less than fair market value, avoiding adverse tax under Section 409A for US-taxpayer option holders.
How long is a 409A valuation valid?
Generally up to 12 months, unless a material event — such as a priced funding round — happens earlier and requires a refresh.
Can a 409A valuation be used for Indian ESOP tax?
No. For employees in India, fair market value of unlisted shares on exercise must be determined by a SEBI-registered Category I merchant banker, with a valuation date no more than 180 days before exercise.
Who deducts tax when an employee in India exercises US parent options?
The Indian employer deducts TDS on the perquisite through payroll, based on the merchant-banker fair market value.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
