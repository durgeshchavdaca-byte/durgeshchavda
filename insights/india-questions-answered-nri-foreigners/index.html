FAST-DS for Indians abroad, by country (20 November 2026)

Do NRIs in the UK need to file FAST-DS?

Only for years they were resident in India while holding a UK account, ISA, pension or shares that was not reported, such as the year of moving or years after returning. People who were non-resident throughout have nothing to declare. Read more.

Do NRIs in the USA need to file FAST-DS?

Only for resident years in India in which US assets were not reported. The most common case is RSUs or ESPP shares that vested while working in India, usually regularised at a flat Rs 1 lakh. Read more.

Do NRIs in the UAE need to file FAST-DS?

Not for years as a non-resident. Families who returned to India and became ordinarily resident but kept UAE bank accounts, companies or property unreported can use FAST-DS, usually at a flat Rs 1 lakh. Read more.

Do NRIs in Australia need to file FAST-DS?

Only for years they were resident in India while holding an Australian bank account, superannuation or shares that was not reported, including the year a student left India. Read more.

Do NRIs in Singapore need to file FAST-DS?

Only for resident years in India in which Singapore accounts, CPF, SRS or company shares were not reported, for example after moving back to India. Read more.

Do NRIs in Europe need to file FAST-DS?

Only for years they were resident in India while holding a European account or asset that was not reported. Student blocked accounts opened in the year of leaving India are a common case, often within the Rs 20 lakh relief. Read more.

Do Indian students in Russia need to file FAST-DS?

Only if they were resident in India in a year when they held the Russian account and did not report it. Many student accounts are small and fall within the Rs 20 lakh relief. Read more.

Do Indian families in Africa need to file FAST-DS?

Family members who live in India and co-own shares, are directors or can sign on an account in Africa had to report those interests. If they did not, FAST-DS can regularise them before 31 December 2026. Read more.

Deadlines and notices: late November 2026 (20 November 2026)

What is the ITR due date for companies and audit cases for AY 2026-27?

21 November 2026, after the CBDT extension from 31 October; it has now passed, so a belated return can be filed until 31 December 2026. Transfer-pricing cases were not extended and file by 30 November 2026. Read more.

What is the last date to claim GST input tax credit for FY 2025-26?

30 November 2026, or the date the GSTR-9 annual return is filed if earlier. The same date applies to correcting GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B and declaring credit notes for that year. Read more.

How can an NRI pensioner submit the life certificate from abroad?

By 30 November 2026, through Jeevan Pramaan face authentication, or with a certificate signed by an Indian embassy or consulate, a notary, a magistrate or a bank officer, sent to the pension-paying bank. Read more.

Does an Indian subsidiary of a foreign group have to file Form 3CEAA?

Yes. Part A of the master file is required from every constituent entity of an international group, by 30 November 2026 for FY 2025-26. Part B applies only above the Rs 500 crore group revenue and Rs 50 crore transaction thresholds. Read more.

How many days do I have to respond to a 143(1)(a) proposed adjustment?

30 days. A defective return notice under section 139(9) must be cured within 15 days, or the return is treated as invalid. Read more.

Why are taxpayers getting notices on refund claims in November 2026?

The income tax department has flagged 11,458 high-risk refund claims worth about Rs 966 crore, mostly false allowances and deductions. A wrong claim for FY 2025-26 can be fixed with a revised return, fee-free until 31 December 2026. Read more.

When does an acquisition in India need CCI approval?

When the parties or group cross the section 5 asset or turnover thresholds and the target is not covered by the de minimis exemption, or when the deal value exceeds Rs 2,000 crore and the target has substantial business operations in India. Read more.

What stake gives a foreign partner a veto in an Indian company?

More than 25% of voting rights, because special resolutions need at least three times as many votes in favour as against. 75% lets a shareholder pass special resolutions alone. Read more.

Overseas subsidiaries: APR and structure questions (19 November 2026)

Does a wholly owned overseas subsidiary need audited accounts for the APR?

Yes. The APR must be based on audited financial statements. Unaudited accounts certified by the Indian entity's statutory auditor or a chartered accountant are allowed only where the Indian investor does not have control and the host country does not require an audit. Read more.

When is the APR due?

By 31 December every year. Where the foreign entity's accounting year ends on 31 December, the APR is due by 31 December of the following year. Read more.

What is the late fee for a delayed APR?

Rs 7,500 per return under RBI's late submission fee matrix (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 16, 30 September 2022), available up to three years from the due date. Read more.

Can an Indian company's overseas subsidiary invest back into India?

Yes, but the structure must not have more than two layers of subsidiaries, at the time of investment or afterwards, under Rule 19(3) of the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022. Banks and certain NBFCs are exempt. Read more.

Deals, entry and set-up: foreign companies ask (19 November 2026)

Can a foreign company use a virtual office as its registered office in India?

For correspondence, yes, but a mail-only virtual office often fails GST physical verification and bank checks. A serviced office or co-working desk with a written agreement and utility bill in the company's name is safer. Read more.

What is a quality of earnings review for an Indian company?

A buy-side financial review that restates EBITDA to a normalised, recurring level, sets a normal working capital level and lists debt-like items. In Indian targets the biggest adjustments usually come from related-party transactions, GST and TDS gaps and pending tax demands. Read more.

Should a foreign buyer buy shares or the business of an Indian company?

Buying shares is simplest and keeps licences in place, but the buyer inherits all past liabilities. Buying the business (a slump sale) leaves most liabilities behind but needs an Indian buyer entity and moves licences, contracts and staff across. A merger needs a tribunal-approved scheme. Read more.

How do foreign companies research the Indian market?

By combining import-export and industry data, competitors' financial statements filed with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, store, price and distributor checks, and a map of licences and city-level costs. A focused study takes four to eight weeks. Read more.

What do foreign companies hire Indian consultants for?

Market research, choosing and setting up an entity, a compliant place of business, bank accounts and capital, due diligence on partners, suppliers and targets, deal structuring, joint ventures, licensing, and ongoing tax and compliance. Read more.

How long does an Indian company have to issue shares after receiving foreign investment?

Within 60 days of receiving the money, or the amount must be refunded within the next 15 days. Form FC-GPR is then due within 30 days of issuing the shares. Read more.

Has the India-US trade deal been signed?

Not as of 18 November 2026. A February 2026 joint statement set out a framework for an interim agreement and talks continued in New Delhi in early November. US buyers should confirm the current duty on their product with a customs broker. Read more.

New rules foreign companies are asking about (18 November 2026)

Does India's DPDP Act apply to a foreign company with no Indian entity?

Yes, if it processes digital personal data outside India in connection with offering goods or services to people in India (section 3(b) of the DPDP Act, 2023). The main obligations under the DPDP Rules, 2025 apply from 13 May 2027. Read more.

Does the DPDP Act apply when we outsource work on non-Indian customer data to an Indian vendor?

Mostly no. Section 17(1)(d) exempts processing of personal data of people outside India by an India-based vendor under a contract with a foreign company from most of the Act, but reasonable security safeguards still apply. Read more.

Does a foreign brand need EPR registration to sell in India?

If its products or packaging fall under the plastic packaging, e-waste, battery or tyre EPR rules, an India-based producer, importer or brand owner must register with CPCB before import or sale, usually the Indian subsidiary or the importer. Read more.

Should my Indian distributor hold our EPR registration?

It can, as importer, but then your ability to keep selling depends on that partner. If it does, the distribution agreement should cover EPR returns, certificates, data access and handover if you switch distributors. Read more.

Can India tax my UK company if I run it from India?

Only if its place of effective management is in India (section 6(10) of the Income-tax Act, 2025). CBDT guidance does not apply this test to foreign companies with turnover of Rs 50 crore or less, but running the business from India can still create a permanent establishment. Read more.

Can I keep my foreign company shares after moving back to India?

Yes. Section 6(4) of FEMA lets a resident keep foreign assets acquired while non-resident, and income from them. New investment into the company after becoming resident follows the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022. Read more.

Has the India-EU free trade agreement been signed?

Not yet, as of 18 November 2026. Negotiations concluded on 27 January 2026 and on 11 September 2026 the European Commission asked the EU Council to approve signing and conclusion. Signing is targeted for the end of 2026, with entry into force expected in 2027. Read more.

What foreign companies ask Indian advisers most (November 2026)

How can a US company set up a wholly owned subsidiary in India, and how long does it take?

Incorporate a private limited company through SPICe+ with at least two directors (one resident in India), open a bank account, bring in capital and file Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotting shares. With documents ready, incorporation usually takes two to four weeks. Read more.

Do I need an Indian resident director to register a company in India as a foreigner?

Yes. Every Indian company needs at least one director who stayed in India for 182 days or more in the year. The director carries legal responsibility, so choose someone with a real role rather than a name-only nominee. Read more.

Does a remote employee in India create a permanent establishment for my foreign company?

It can if the employee concludes or negotiates contracts, manages the business or works from a fixed place at the company's disposal. Purely technical or back-office work carries lower risk; long arrangements are usually moved to an employer of record or an Indian subsidiary. Read more.

My Indian client is deducting 20% tax from my invoice. How do I get the treaty rate?

Give the Indian payer a tax residency certificate from your country and Form 41 (which replaced Form 10F from April 2026), and confirm you have no permanent establishment in India. The payer can then apply the lower treaty rate. Read more.

Do I need to register for GST in India if I sell SaaS to Indian consumers from abroad?

Yes. Foreign providers of online digital services to Indian consumers must register under the simplified OIDAR scheme with no threshold, and file monthly returns. Sales to GST-registered Indian businesses are taxed under reverse charge by the buyer. Read more.

How do I verify that an Indian supplier is genuine before paying an advance?

Check that the same legal name appears on its GST registration, Import Export Code and company record, that the bank account is in that name, and ask for a live video tour. For larger orders, get an independent site visit. Read more.

What annual compliance does a foreign-owned company in India need?

Statutory audit, annual ROC filings, income tax return, the FLA return to the RBI, transfer pricing documentation and the accountant's report on international transactions (Form 48, formerly Form 3CEB), GST returns and TDS filings. Read more.

Do UK employees posted to India still pay UK National Insurance?

Since 15 July 2026, under the India-UK Double Contributions Convention, posted employees can stay in the UK system for up to 60 months with an HMRC certificate of coverage, and are then exempt from Indian provident fund for that period. Read more.

Do foreign manufacturers need an Indian representative for BIS certification?

Yes. Under the BIS Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme, the foreign manufacturer must nominate an Authorised Indian Representative who is resident in India, and keep one in place for as long as the licence runs. Read more.

Can a foreign company run an online gaming business in India?

Online money games are banned under the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 and its Rules in force since 1 May 2026, and foreign investment in gambling and betting is prohibited. E-sports, social games and game studios remain open. Read more.

For foreign companies working with Indian partners: mid-November 2026

How can a foreign company recover an unpaid invoice from an Indian customer?

Check the buyer, get the debt acknowledged in writing and send a formal demand. Then: settlement or mediation (mandatory before a commercial suit of Rs 3 lakh or more), an IBC demand if the undisputed debt is Rs 1 crore or more, arbitration if the contract has a clause, or a suit. You generally have 3 years from the due date. Read more.

Can a US court judgment be enforced in India?

Not directly. The US is not a reciprocating territory under section 44A of the Code of Civil Procedure, so a fresh suit on the judgment is needed. UK, Singapore and UAE judgments can be executed directly. Read more.

Is a foreign employee's salary taxable in India on a short trip?

Exempt if the employee is a foreign citizen, the employer is a foreign enterprise not doing business in India, the stay is 90 days or less in the tax year and the salary is not charged to an Indian company. Treaties usually extend relief to stays under 183 days. Read more.

Do foreign employees in India pay provident fund on their full salary?

Yes, as international workers they contribute on full salary with no wage ceiling, unless they hold a certificate of coverage from a country with a social security agreement with India. Read more.

Do you have to compensate an Indian distributor when you terminate?

There is no statutory goodwill indemnity for distributors in India; the contract decides. Without the contractual or reasonable notice, the distributor can claim damages, but usually cannot force the relationship to continue. Read more.

When does a foreign company have a permanent establishment in India?

Typically with a fixed place of business, a dependent agent who habitually concludes contracts, staff providing services beyond the treaty limit (90 days in 12 months under the US, UK and Singapore treaties), or a long project. PE profits are taxed at 35% plus surcharge and cess. Read more.

What withholding tax applies to royalties paid by an Indian company?

20% plus surcharge and cess under Indian law, or the lower treaty rate (often 10% or 15%) with a TRC and Form 41. There is no RBI cap on royalty; GST of 18% applies under reverse charge for the Indian payer. Read more.

Can part of the price for Indian shares be paid later as an earn-out?

Yes. Up to 25% of the total consideration can be deferred, escrowed or paid as an indemnity for up to 18 months from the agreement date under the FEMA non-debt instrument rules. Read more.

How does a foreign company close its Indian subsidiary?

A company with no business and no liabilities can apply for strike-off (Form STK-2); otherwise use voluntary liquidation under the IBC, or sell the shares (Form FC-TRS). Clear tax, GST and FEMA filings and repatriate funds first. Read more.

What triggers an open offer when a foreigner buys into an Indian listed company?

Acquiring 25% or more of voting rights, or control, triggers a mandatory open offer for at least 26% more under SEBI takeover rules. Holders of 25% or more can add up to 5% a year without an offer. Read more.

For foreign companies: November 2026 updates

Can a fund with Chinese investors invest in India without government approval?

Yes, if land-border-country beneficial ownership in the investor is 10% or less and carries no control, and the sector is on the automatic route (Press Note 2, 2026). Above 10% or with control, approval is still needed. Read more.

How much of an Indian bank or NBFC can a foreign investor own?

Private banks: up to 74% foreign investment (above 49% with government approval), and RBI approval to reach 5%. NBFCs: up to 100% automatic, but RBI approval for control, 26% or more, or a change of over 30% of directors. Read more.

When does the EU deforestation regulation apply to imports from India?

From 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators (30 June 2027 for micro and small, outside timber). Coffee, rubber, wood, soya, cocoa, palm and cattle products need plot-level geolocation. Read more.

When do EU importers pay CBAM on Indian steel?

Certificates for 2026 imports go on sale on 1 February 2027, and the first annual declaration is due by 30 September 2027. Without verified supplier data, default values apply. Read more.

Does the UK failure to prevent fraud offence cover Indian agents and subsidiaries?

It can. Since 1 September 2025, large organisations are liable for fraud by associated persons, including agents, subsidiaries and service providers in India, unless they had reasonable prevention procedures. Read more.

What benefits must a foreign employer give staff in India?

PF at 12% (mandatory up to ₹25,000 a month in wages since 17 September 2026), ESI for lower earners, gratuity, statutory bonus, 26 weeks' maternity leave and state paid leave. Read more.

Can a foreign brand own its stores in India?

Yes. Single-brand retail allows 100% FDI on the automatic route; above 51%, 30% of goods must be sourced from India. Franchising or licensing needs no FDI approval. Read more.

I own a US LLC but never filed Form FC. What now?

File Form FC through your bank to get a UIN, pay a late submission fee if within the allowed window or apply for compounding, then file the missed APRs and fix Schedule FA. Read more.

How much tax does an NRI in Dubai pay on selling property in India?

Indian tax only: 12.5% on long-term gains plus surcharge and cess. The UAE has no personal income tax. Get a lower TDS certificate (Form 128) so TDS isn't deducted on the full price. Read more.

Do UK residents pay tax on selling property in India?

Yes: UK capital gains tax at 18% or 24% on the gain in pounds, after the £3,000 annual exempt amount, with credit for the Indian tax. Read more.

Do freelancers still need to file an EDF for foreign payments?

Not separately. Since 1 October 2026, a resident individual's EDF is deemed filed once the bank has the payment details; businesses get the same up to ₹10 lakh per remittance. Read more.

For NRIs: tax

Do NRIs have to pay tax in India?

Yes, on Indian income only — rent, NRO interest, dividends and capital gains on Indian assets. Income earned and received abroad isn't taxed in India. Read more.

What are the NRI tax slabs for 2026-27?

The same as residents under the new regime (nil to ₹4 lakh, then 5% to 30%), but without the rebate that makes ₹12 lakh tax-free for residents. Read more.

Is NRE interest taxable?

No. NRE and FCNR interest is tax-free for NRIs; NRO interest is taxable. Read more.

Do NRIs need to file an Indian tax return?

Yes if Indian income is above the basic exemption — and usually worth it to recover excess TDS. Read more.

How many days in India make an NRI resident?

Generally 182 days in a year. For Indian citizens and people of Indian origin visiting India, the shorter test applies only if Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh — 120 days plus 365 days over the previous four years. Read more.

What is FAST-DS 2026?

A one-time window until 31 December 2026 to declare undisclosed foreign assets (60% cost up to ₹1 crore) or assets missed in Schedule FA (₹1 lakh up to ₹5 crore). Read more.

For NRIs: money and property

How much can an NRI repatriate from India?

Up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO accounts after tax, with Forms 145 and 146; NRE balances are fully repatriable. Read more.

Does the US 1% remittance tax apply to bank transfers to India?

No — only to transfers funded with cash, money orders or cashier's checks, from 1 January 2026. Read more.

How much TDS applies when an NRI sells property?

TDS at non-resident capital-gains rates — buyers often deduct on the full price, far more than the final tax; a lower-deduction certificate (Form 128) fixes that. Read more.

Does a buyer need a TAN to buy property from an NRI?

Not since 1 October 2026 for resident individual and HUF buyers — they can deposit TDS using their PAN through Form 141. Read more.

Can NRIs buy agricultural land?

No. NRIs and OCIs can buy homes and commercial property but can only inherit agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses. Read more.

Can NRIs apply for IPOs in India?

Yes — through ASBA from an NRE or NRO account, holding shares in an NRI demat account; check each offer document. Read more.

For foreign individuals

Can a foreigner buy property in India?

A foreign national living outside India who isn't an OCI can only inherit Indian property or lease it for up to 5 years. Read more.

Can foreigners invest in Indian shares?

Yes. Since 12 June 2026 any individual resident outside India can buy listed Indian shares directly, without FPI registration, below 10% per company. Read more.

Can OCI cardholders buy property in India?

Yes — residential and commercial, but not agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses. Read more.

Do foreign clients deduct tax when paying Indian freelancers?

Generally no, if the foreign client has no Indian presence; the freelancer treats it as an export of services. Read more.

For foreign companies: entering India

Can a foreign company own 100% of an Indian company?

Yes in most sectors under the automatic route; some sectors and land-border-country investors need approval. Read more.

Subsidiary, branch or liaison office?

Most choose a subsidiary (22% base tax option); a branch pays 35% plus surcharge; a liaison office can't earn income. Read more.

Can we hire in India without an entity?

Yes, through an employer of record — but staff who conclude contracts can create permanent-establishment risk. Read more.

What changed in Press Note 3 in 2026?

Investors with non-controlling beneficial ownership up to 10% from land-border countries can use the automatic route. Read more.

Is the H-1B $100,000 fee being collected?

Not as of October 2026 — a court vacated it in June 2026 — but appeals and a 2026 extension proclamation leave it unsettled. Read more.

What does a GCC in India cost in tax?

It's taxed on its cost-plus markup, usually at the 22% base option, with services to the parent zero-rated for GST. Read more.

For foreign companies: tax and money

Why is my Indian customer deducting 20%?

Indian law requires withholding on royalties and technical fees paid abroad; your treaty rate applies with a TRC, Form 41 and a no-PE declaration. Read more.

What replaced Form 10F?

Form 41, under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Read more.

Does a foreign company need to file an Indian tax return?

Usually yes if it has Indian taxable income — unless its only income is dividends, interest, royalties or fees taxed at the full domestic rate. Read more.

How are dividends to a foreign parent taxed?

20% plus surcharge and cess, or the treaty rate — 15% for a US parent with 10%+, 10% for UK, UAE, Japanese, German or Dutch parents. Read more.

Does foreign SaaS need Indian GST?

Yes for sales to consumers and unregistered businesses — simplified registration, 18% IGST; B2B sales are reverse charge. Read more.

Is India's equalisation levy still charged?

No — the 2% levy ended on 1 August 2024 and the 6% levy on online ads on 1 April 2025. Read more.

Checking and buying Indian companies

How do I verify an Indian company?

Check MCA master data, the GST taxpayer search and the IEC (all free), then read its filed accounts on MCA for about ₹100. Read more.

Are private companies' accounts public in India?

Yes — financial statements and annual returns are filed with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs and can be viewed for a small fee. Read more.

What's the biggest due-diligence red flag in India?

Foreign investment that was never reported to RBI or was issued without a valuation — followed by GST mismatches and open tax demands. Read more.

How is a share purchase from an Indian seller priced?

At or above fair value under FEMA, certified by a CA, merchant banker or cost accountant, with FC-TRS within 60 days. Read more.

Trade deals

Is the India–UK trade deal in force?

Yes — CETA took effect on 15 July 2026, with a 5-year social-security exemption for posted employees. Read more.

Is the India–New Zealand FTA in force?

Yes — since 20 October 2026. Read more.

Has India signed a trade deal with the EU?

Negotiations concluded on 27 January 2026; signing is expected in December 2026, with entry into force indicated for February–March 2027. Read more.

Is the India–EFTA (Switzerland) agreement in force?

Yes, since 1 October 2025. Read more.
