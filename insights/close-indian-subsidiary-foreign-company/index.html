Exiting India: three ways to close or sell an Indian subsidiary

Plans change: a market entry stalls, a JV partner exits, or a group restructures. Closing an Indian private company takes longer than closing a US or UK entity, and the money only leaves India once the paperwork is right. Here are the three routes, when each fits, and the steps for getting funds back to the parent.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 15 October 2026·6 min read
Plan your exit hello@durgeshchavda.com
Key takeaways
  • Strike-off (STK-2) for dormant, clean companies.
  • Voluntary liquidation (IBC section 59) for solvent companies with assets.
  • Clear tax, GST, staff and FEMA filings before repatriating funds.
Quick answer: a foreign parent can (1) strike off the company by filing Form STK-2 if it has had no business for two financial years and has no liabilities, (2) put a solvent company into voluntary liquidation under section 59 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, with a liquidator and an NCLT dissolution order, or (3) sell the shares to an Indian or foreign buyer. Before any route: clear tax, GST, employee and creditor dues. Remaining funds are repatriated through the company’s bank under FEMA.

Before you close: the clean-up list

  • Tax: file pending income-tax returns, close open assessments and notices, and settle TDS filings.
  • GST: file final returns and cancel the GST registration.
  • Employees: full and final settlements, gratuity, leave encashment, and closing PF and ESI accounts.
  • Contracts and leases: terminate office leases, vendor and customer contracts; collect receivables.
  • FEMA: bring overdue filings up to date (FC-GPR, FC-TRS, the annual FLA return), with late fees if needed.
  • Intercompany balances: settle or waive loans and payables with the parent, considering the tax effect of any waiver.
  • Records: keep books and records for the period the law requires after closure.

Getting the money out

MoneyHow it goes back
Surplus on liquidationRemitted to the foreign shareholder through the authorised dealer bank, with the liquidator’s and auditor’s documents and the tax position confirmed
Sale proceedsPaid by the buyer to the foreign seller after tax withholding; FC-TRS filed
Loans from the parentRepaid under the original loan terms before closure
Dividends before closurePaid out of profits with withholding tax at treaty rates; see repatriating profits from India

Branch and liaison offices

A branch, liaison or project office is closed through the authorised dealer bank that approved it: file pending annual activity certificates and tax returns, settle liabilities, and submit the closure documents so the bank can permit the remittance of the remaining funds. Frequently asked questions

How can a foreign company close its Indian subsidiary?
By striking off the company with Form STK-2 if it has had no business for two financial years and has no liabilities, by voluntary liquidation under section 59 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code if it is solvent but has assets or obligations to wind up, or by selling the shares to a buyer.
What is Form STK-2?
Form STK-2 is the application to the Registrar of Companies to strike a company's name off the register under section 248(2) of the Companies Act, 2013. It is filed with a fee of Rs 10,000 and processed by the Centre for Processing Accelerated Corporate Exit (C-PACE).
Can the foreign parent repatriate money when an Indian subsidiary is wound up?
Yes. The surplus after paying all creditors and taxes can be remitted to the foreign shareholder through the authorised dealer bank, with supporting documents such as the liquidator's report, an auditor's certificate and confirmation of the tax position.
Do we need to clear FEMA filings before closing an Indian company?
Yes. Overdue FC-GPR, FC-TRS and annual FLA filings should be completed, with late submission fees where needed, because the bank will review the investment history before allowing the final remittance.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
