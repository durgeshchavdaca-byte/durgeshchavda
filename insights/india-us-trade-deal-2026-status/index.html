Sourcing from India for the US? Where the trade deal stands, and what to do meanwhile

US importers keep asking the same question: when will the India–US trade deal be done, and what will it change for goods made in India? Here is the position on 18 November 2026, without the speculation, and the steps worth taking now so you are ready either way.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 18 November 2026·4 min read

Key takeaways
  • Not signed as of 18 Nov 2026; February 2026 framework, talks in final stages.
  • Confirm today's duty for your HTS code; add a tariff-change clause.
  • Origin, transshipment and forced-labour checks matter either way.
Quick answer: as of 18 November 2026 the India–US trade agreement has not been signed. A US–India joint statement in February 2026 set out a framework for an interim agreement, and negotiators met in New Delhi on 2–3 November on customs and trade facilitation. On 14 November India’s External Affairs Minister said most issues had been resolved. US tariff rates and their legal basis changed during 2026, so confirm the current duty on your product with your customs broker before pricing any order.

What US buyers should do now

  1. Price on today’s duty, not the expected one. Get the current rate for your HTS code from your customs broker and build a tariff-change clause into supply contracts.
  2. Get origin right. Duty depends on where goods are made, and US Customs looks at whether the Indian process substantially transforms imported inputs. Ask suppliers for a bill of materials and process flow.
  3. Stay clear of transshipment. Goods routed through India to avoid duties on another country can trigger a US Customs evasion investigation under the Enforce and Protect Act. Visit the factory, check capacity and match it to the volumes offered.
  4. Check forced-labour exposure. Cotton, polysilicon, aluminium and other inputs may need traceability under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act.
  5. Diligence the supplier itself. Same legal name on GST, Import Export Code and company records, bank account in that name, real factory, clean litigation and tax record.

If the deal is signed

Expect rules of origin, certificates and supplier declarations to matter more, because preferential rates usually require proof that goods qualify. Suppliers with clean records and documented processes will be able to give that proof. Others will not. Checking now saves a scramble later.

Frequently asked questions

Has the India–US trade deal been signed?
Not as of 18 November 2026. A US–India joint statement in February 2026 set out a framework for an interim agreement, talks continued in New Delhi in early November, and India's External Affairs Minister said on 14 November that most issues had been resolved.
What US tariff applies to goods from India now?
US tariff measures and their legal basis changed several times during 2026, so the rate depends on the product and the date of entry. Confirm the current duty for your HTS code with a licensed customs broker before pricing orders.
What should US companies check before sourcing from India?
That the supplier is genuine (matching GST, IEC and company records, bank account in its own name), that it really manufactures the goods at the volumes offered, how origin is documented, and whether any inputs carry forced-labour risk under the UFLPA.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
