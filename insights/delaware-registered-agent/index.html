Delaware registered agent: the address that keeps your company alive

Every Delaware company must keep a registered agent at all times. For founders in India, it’s also the channel through which lawsuits and state notices reach you — so the choice matters more than the price.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 6 October 2026·6 min read
Get Delaware-compliant hello@durgeshchavda.com

What is a registered agent?

A registered agent is the person or company your Delaware corporation or LLC appoints to receive legal papers and official notices on its behalf, at a physical street address in Delaware (the “registered office”). You name the agent in your Certificate of Incorporation or Formation, and the company must keep one continuously for as long as it exists.

Who can act as a registered agent?

  • An individual who lives in Delaware, or
  • A business authorised to act as a registered agent in Delaware — the route almost every foreign-owned company uses.

The address must be a real street address where papers can be delivered during business hours. A P.O. box, or a virtual office or mail-forwarding address on its own, won’t do. Founders based in India therefore can’t act as their own agent.

What the agent does for you

  • Service of process — accepts lawsuits and legal documents and forwards them to you promptly.
  • State correspondence — franchise-tax and annual-report notices from the Delaware Division of Corporations.
  • Compliance reminders — most commercial agents remind you about the 1 March annual report for corporations or the 1 June LLC tax.
  • Public address — the agent’s address appears on the public record instead of yours.

How to choose one from India

  1. Fast, digital forwarding — scanned documents emailed the same day, so a lawsuit deadline doesn’t pass while papers sit in a mailbox.
  2. Clear, all-in pricing — check renewal fees and whether forwarding or compliance alerts cost extra.
  3. Delaware filing support — many agents can file your annual report or amendments, which helps from another time zone.
  4. Longevity — an agent that disappears or resigns creates a 30-day scramble (see below).

Changing your registered agent

You can switch at any time by filing a certificate of change of registered agent (or an amendment) with the Delaware Division of Corporations, usually handled by the new agent. Delaware revised many of its filing fees from 1 August 2026 under House Bill 400, so confirm the current fee when you file.

If your registered agent resigns

You have 30 days. If an agent resigns without a successor being appointed, the company must designate a new Delaware registered agent within 30 days of the resignation filing. Otherwise a corporation’s charter can be forfeited and an LLC’s certificate of formation cancelled.

Agents most often resign because their own fees went unpaid — so keep a working email on file and pay renewals on time. Reviving a forfeited or cancelled company is possible but costs more than staying compliant, and loses good standing in the meantime.

Watch out for scam notices

The Division of Corporations warns Delaware entities about deceptive letters and emails that look official. Treat any bill that doesn’t come directly from the State or your own registered agent with suspicion, and check before paying.

What a registered agent costs — and what drives it

  • The annual fee is the core cost, charged by the private provider (Delaware doesn’t set it). Compare the renewal price, not just the first-year offer.
  • Mail forwarding and scanning — some agents only forward service of process and state notices; scanning general mail is often an add-on.
  • Compliance alerts and filings — reminders for the 1 March annual report and franchise tax (corporations) or the 1 June LLC tax, and whether they’ll file for you.
  • Bundles — formation packages often include the first year free and recover it at renewal.

How to read registered agent reviews

  • Look for complaints about missed or late notices — the one failure that really costs you (default judgments, lost good standing).
  • Check how easy it is to change agents or cancel, and whether the price rose sharply at renewal.
  • Prefer agents that confirm a physical Delaware address and business-hours availability, as Delaware law requires.
  • For Indian founders, check they can handle notices quickly across time zones — and that someone on your side tracks the Indian filings too (see the compliance calendar).

Frequently asked questions

Is a registered agent mandatory in Delaware?
Yes. Every Delaware corporation and LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in Delaware.
Can I be my own registered agent from India?
No. The agent must have a physical Delaware address where legal papers can be served during business hours, so founders outside Delaware appoint a commercial registered agent.
What happens if my Delaware registered agent resigns?
The company must appoint a new registered agent within 30 days. If it doesn't, a corporation's charter may be forfeited and an LLC's certificate of formation cancelled.
Can I change my registered agent later?
Yes — by filing a certificate of change of registered agent (or an amendment) with the Delaware Division of Corporations, typically through the new agent.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
