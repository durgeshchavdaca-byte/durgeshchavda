Is this Indian supplier legit? Red flags and the checks you can run today

Most Indian exporters are genuine, but buyers from abroad do get caught by fake companies, borrowed identities, traders posing as factories, and emails that swap the bank account at the last minute. Before you send an advance, run these checks. Most take minutes and use free public registers.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 16 November 2026·5 min read
Check your supplier hello@durgeshchavda.com
Key takeaways
  • Same legal name on GST, IEC, company register and bank account.
  • Never act on emailed bank-detail changes without a call.
  • Small first order or letter of credit; site visit for big orders.
Quick answer: check that the exact legal name appears and matches across the supplier’s GST registration (active, returns filed), Import Export Code (IEC), and the company register (MCA) or Udyam registration, and that the bank account is in the same name. Insist on a live video walk-through of the facility, pay a small first order or use a letter of credit, and never act on emailed changes to bank details without calling a number you already had. For larger orders, get an independent site visit before paying.

Paying safely

  • Start with a small trial order; scale up after delivery.
  • For larger orders use a letter of credit, or pay the balance against shipping documents.
  • Pay only to an account in the supplier’s legal name, confirmed on its letterhead and by phone.
  • Use pre-shipment inspection for quality, and an arbitration clause in the contract.

When to get someone on the ground

For a first order above what you can afford to lose, a long-term supply agreement, a JV or an exclusive distribution deal, desk checks are not enough. An independent visit confirms the facility exists and operates, the capacity is real, the people you deal with are who they say they are, and there is no litigation or financial stress. Frequently asked questions

How do I check if an Indian supplier is genuine?
Check that the same legal name appears on its GST registration (active, with returns filed), its Import Export Code and the company register or Udyam registration, and that the bank account is in that name. Ask for a live video tour, start with a small order and, for large orders, arrange an independent site visit.
How can I verify an Indian company's GST number?
Use Search Taxpayer on the GST portal with the GSTIN. It shows the legal and trade name, registration status, date, principal place of business and return filing history. Characters 3 to 12 of the GSTIN are the business's PAN.
Does an Indian exporter need an IEC?
Yes. An Importer Exporter Code issued by the DGFT is needed to export goods from India, and it should be in the same name and PAN as the supplier's GST registration.
What should I do if my Indian supplier emails new bank details?
Do not pay. Call the supplier on a phone number you already had, not one in the email, and confirm the change in writing on letterhead. Last-minute bank changes are a common payment fraud.
