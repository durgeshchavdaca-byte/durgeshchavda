Selling on Amazon US from India: which entity, which taxes?

You can list on Amazon.com as an Indian exporter or through a US company. The choice changes who imports the goods, where profits are taxed and which filings follow — here’s how to decide.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·8 min read
Pick the right structure hello@durgeshchavda.com

Selling as an Indian exporter

  • Registrations: an Importer Exporter Code (IEC) from DGFT, GST registration, an AD code with your bank, and a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) so exports are zero-rated without paying IGST upfront.
  • US customs: goods entering the US need an importer of record who handles duties and clearance.
  • Payment: give Amazon a W-8BEN-E for the Indian entity — see the W-8BEN-E guide.
  • Export proceeds must be realised in India within the period RBI prescribes and matched to your shipping records.

Selling through a US company

  • A US LLC or C-Corp buys stock from you (or your Indian company) and sells it on Amazon.com as a US seller — often preferred by larger brands, and simpler for US customs and US-only programmes.
  • The Indian side becomes an export to a related party: the price must be at arm’s length — see transfer pricing.
  • The US entity brings its own filings: Form 1120 or the foreign-owned LLC return with Form 5472, Delaware or state fees, and ODI reporting in India.
  • Choose the entity type with care — C-Corp vs LLC — especially if the LLC’s owners live in India (why).

US sales tax

Every US state with a sales tax now has a marketplace facilitator law, so Amazon calculates, collects and remits sales tax on sales it facilitates. You generally don’t collect sales tax on those Amazon orders yourself — but sales through your own website are a separate matter. See US sales tax.

FBA and US income tax

Storing inventory in Amazon warehouses is where the US income-tax question arises for Indian exporters. Under the India–US treaty, a foreign business is taxed on business profits in the US only if it has a permanent establishment there. The treaty excludes facilities used solely for storage, display or occasional delivery of goods — and whether regular fulfilment from US warehouses stays within that exclusion depends on the facts. Sellers with significant FBA volumes should get this assessed rather than assume “no US tax”.

A US company avoids the question — it simply pays US tax on the US profit — but then the transfer price between India and the US decides how much profit is taxed where.

Getting paid

  • Indian exporter: receive Amazon disbursements into your Indian bank account through channels that generate proper inward-remittance evidence, and reconcile them against shipping bills.
  • US company: the US entity’s bank receives the payouts; money reaches India as payment for goods supplied, or as dividends. See US bank account from India.

How to decide

  • Testing the market or small volumes: sell as an Indian exporter.
  • Large FBA inventory, US team or US retail channels: a US company usually makes more sense.
  • Brand building: protect the brand in the US first — see US trademark.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Indian business sell on Amazon.com without a US company?
Yes. Through Amazon Global Selling, an Indian business can sell on Amazon.com as an exporter, typically with an IEC, GST registration, an LUT for zero-rated exports, an AD code and arrangements for a US importer of record.
Do I need to collect US sales tax on Amazon sales?
Generally no for orders Amazon facilitates: every US state with a sales tax has a marketplace facilitator law requiring Amazon to collect and remit sales tax on those sales.
Does FBA inventory in the US create US income tax for an Indian seller?
It can. Under the India–US treaty, US tax on business profits depends on having a permanent establishment; the treaty excludes facilities used solely for storage, display or occasional delivery, so regular FBA fulfilment should be assessed on its facts.
Should I use a US LLC or C-Corp for Amazon US?
It depends on volumes, investors and where the owners live. A C-Corp is simpler for India-resident owners in many cases; an LLC owned from India brings Form 5472 filings and Indian tax-classification issues.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
