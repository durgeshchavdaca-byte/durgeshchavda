Sending OTP SMS to Indian users from abroad? How DLT works for foreign companies

If your app has users in India, your login OTPs must pass India’s DLT (distributed ledger) system, or telecom operators block or filter them. DLT registration expects an Indian business behind the messages, which is where foreign companies get stuck. Here are your realistic options, what each involves, and the tax and FEMA side that most guides skip.

Key takeaways
  • OTP SMS to India need a DLT-registered Indian principal entity.
  • Options: own entity, Indian partner with brand authorisation, provider route, non-SMS OTP.
  • Partner route can be zero-rated for GST; the 2026 intermediary rule change helps.
Quick answer: commercial SMS to Indian numbers, including OTPs, must come from a principal entity registered on DLT, using an approved sender header (generally six characters tied to the entity’s brand) and registered message templates. Registration needs Indian business documents, so a foreign company usually needs one of four routes: its own Indian entity, an Indian partner or contractor registering as principal entity with a brand authorisation, an SMS provider’s pre-approved OTP route, or non-SMS OTPs (WhatsApp, email, authenticator apps). Entity approval typically takes about a week and header approval a few days more.

DLT steps once you have an Indian principal entity

  1. Entity registration on an operator’s DLT portal with PAN, GST or other business registration and authorised signatory details (with verification).
  2. Header registration: a sender ID, generally six characters, that matches the entity’s name or brand. If the brand belongs to your foreign company, upload a brand authorisation or trademark document.
  3. Template registration: each OTP message format, with variables, approved in advance.
  4. Link to your SMS provider (the telemarketer chain), then send only through registered headers and templates.

The tax and FEMA side (option 2)

  • Export of services: the Indian business invoicing your foreign company can zero-rate the service under an LUT if the export conditions are met, and claim a refund of the 18% GST it pays on SMS purchases.
  • The 2026 intermediary change: from 30 March 2026 the Finance Act 2026 removed the special place-of-supply rule for intermediary services (IGST section 13(8)(b)). Even an arranging-type service now follows the recipient’s location, so it can be zero-rated if the other export conditions are met. A clear principal-to-principal contract still helps with documentation and refund claims. See GST on export of services.
  • Payments: advance payments for exports are allowed; receipts come through the Indian bank with an e-FIRC.
  • Your own exposure: an independent contractor providing a service to you does not normally create a permanent establishment, but avoid giving them authority to conclude contracts in your name.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company register on DLT in India?
Generally not directly, because DLT principal entity registration asks for Indian business documents such as PAN and GST or company registration. Foreign companies usually register through their own Indian entity, an Indian partner acting as principal entity with brand authorisation, or an SMS provider's pre-approved OTP route.
Do OTP SMS need DLT registration in India?
Yes. All commercial SMS to Indian numbers, including OTPs and transactional messages, must go through a registered principal entity, an approved sender header and registered templates, or operators block or filter them.
How long does DLT registration take?
Entity registration typically takes about 3 to 7 days and header registration 1 to 3 days after that, with templates approved separately. Getting the Indian entity's GST registration first can add one to two weeks.
Is GST payable when an Indian contractor provides an OTP SMS service to a foreign company?
It can be zero-rated as an export of services under an LUT if the export conditions are met, and the contractor can claim a refund of GST paid on SMS purchases. Since 30 March 2026 the special place-of-supply rule for intermediary services no longer applies, so the recipient's location outside India decides the place of supply.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
