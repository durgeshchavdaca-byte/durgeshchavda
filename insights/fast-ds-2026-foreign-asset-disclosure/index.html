FAST-DS 2026: clean up undisclosed foreign assets by 31 December

Foreign bank accounts, RSUs and ESOPs from a US or UK employer, a dormant account from student days — India’s tax department now sees them through global data exchange. FAST-DS is a one-time window to put them right at a known cost.

What FAST-DS is

The Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme, 2026 (Chapter IV of the Finance Act, 2026; rules notified on 14 August 2026) is a one-time window to declare foreign assets and income that were never reported in India, at a fixed cost and with immunity under the Black Money Act. It opened on 16 August 2026 and closes on 31 December 2026. Values are taken as on 31 March 2026.

Why now: foreign account data received under CRS and FATCA is showing up against PANs, and many taxpayers are getting messages about foreign assets missing from Schedule FA.

Who can use it

  • Anyone who was resident in India in the year the foreign income arose or the foreign asset was acquired.
  • That includes people who are NRIs or RNORs today: you state your residential status for the relevant year in Form 1.
  • It covers cases where you did not file a return, filed but left the asset or income out, or the income escaped assessment.
  • Not available for proceeds of crime where money-laundering proceedings have started, or for years already assessed under the Black Money Act.

The two categories

What it coversA foreign asset whose source you cannot explain, or foreign income that was taxable in India but never offered to taxA foreign asset bought from income already taxed, or bought while you were a non-resident, but left out of Schedule FA
LimitAsset value plus income up to ₹1 crore in aggregateAsset value up to ₹5 crore in aggregate
Cost30% tax plus an equal amount: 60%Flat ₹1,00,000
Typical caseUnreported foreign rent, dividends, interest or sale gains while resident; an account funded from money that escaped Indian taxRSUs taxed through Indian payroll; savings from years abroad; a student-days account

Worked example (from the department’s FAQs): a foreign bank account worth ₹60 lakh and ₹20 lakh of undisclosed foreign income. Tax at 30% is ₹18 lakh plus ₹6 lakh, plus an equal additional amount, so ₹48 lakh in all.

How assets are valued (as on 31 March 2026)

AssetValue
Bank accountSum of all deposits since the account was opened, not the balance (deposits made out of withdrawals from the same account are excluded)
Listed shares, including RSUs and ESPP sharesHigher of cost and the average of the highest and lowest price on 31 March 2026 (or the nearest earlier trading day)
Unlisted shares and interestsHigher of cost and the value under the prescribed formula or a valuer’s report
PropertyHigher of cost and market value, with a valuation report from a valuer recognised in that country
Jewellery, art, other assetsHigher of cost and market value

Foreign currency converts at the RBI reference rate on 31 March 2026 (other currencies go through US dollars first).

How it works: Forms 1 to 4

  1. Form 1: the declaration, filed online with evidence of each asset and its value.
  2. Form 2: the department’s order stating the amount payable.
  3. Payment through challan ITNS 289 (assessment year 2026-27 is pre-filled; a wrong minor head cannot be corrected or refunded) within two months from the end of the month you receive Form 2. Up to two more months are allowed with 1% a month interest; after that the declaration is void.
  4. Form 3: intimation of payment with the proof.
  5. Form 4: the certificate confirming the payment.

FAST-DS or wait and see?

If the department finds it first (Black Money Act)If you declare it under FAST-DS
Undisclosed asset or income: tax at 30% plus a penalty of three times the tax, so 120% of the value, and possible prosecutionCategory 1: 60%, with immunity under the Black Money Act
Asset only missing from Schedule FA: penalty of ₹10 lakh (not applied since 1 October 2024 where your foreign assets other than immovable property total ₹20 lakh or less)Category 2: ₹1 lakh, with immunity

For small, already-taxed holdings under ₹20 lakh, FAST-DS may not be necessary: report them correctly in Schedule FA from now on. For anything larger, or anything with unreported income, FAST-DS is far cheaper than being found later.

Common situations

  • RSUs or ESPP shares from a foreign employer: FAST-DS for RSUs and ESOPs.
  • A foreign bank account left open after studies or work: FAST-DS for foreign bank accounts.
  • Back in India after years abroad: FAST-DS for returning NRIs.

Frequently asked questions

What is FAST-DS 2026?
The Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme, 2026: a one-time window, open from 16 August to 31 December 2026, to declare undisclosed foreign assets or income, or foreign assets left out of Schedule FA, at a fixed cost and with immunity under the Black Money Act.
What is the last date for FAST-DS 2026?
31 December 2026. No declaration can be filed after that date.
How much do you pay under FAST-DS?
For undisclosed foreign assets or income up to ₹1 crore in total: 30% tax plus an equal additional amount, 60% in all. For foreign assets from taxed income or acquired while non-resident, missing only from Schedule FA, up to ₹5 crore: a flat ₹1 lakh.
Can NRIs use FAST-DS?
Yes, if they were resident in India in the year the foreign asset was acquired or the foreign income arose, even if they are non-resident now.
What happens if my foreign assets are worth more than ₹5 crore?
You are not eligible for FAST-DS. Get specific advice on the Black Money Act position.
How is a foreign bank account valued for FAST-DS?
At the total of all deposits made since the account was opened, up to 31 March 2026, excluding deposits made out of withdrawals from the same account. It is not the account balance.
When do I pay after filing FAST-DS?
Within two months from the end of the month in which you receive the Form 2 order. A further two months is allowed with interest at 1% a month; after that the declaration is void.
Does FAST-DS give immunity?
A valid declaration with full payment gives immunity from further tax, penalty and prosecution under the Black Money Act for the assets and income declared.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
