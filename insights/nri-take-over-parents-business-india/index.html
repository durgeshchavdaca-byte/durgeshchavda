An NRI taking over a parent’s business: four ways to do it

A common question from NRIs: a parent has run a sole proprietorship in India for years, wants to step back, and the child abroad wants to take it over, often along with the parent’s savings. The first thing to know is that a proprietorship can’t simply be handed over as an entity. Here are the four routes that work, and what each one means for exchange control, GST and tax.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 15 October 2026·6 min read
Plan the takeover hello@durgeshchavda.com
Key takeaways
  • A proprietorship can't be handed over as an entity; assets must move.
  • NRI as proprietor: non-repatriation only. Company or LLP: profits can go abroad.
  • Cash gift to NRO is tax-free for the child; keep a gift deed.
Quick answer: a sole proprietorship is not a separate legal entity; it is the parent. So the business assets have to move either to the NRI personally (as the new proprietor, allowed only on a non-repatriation basis and not in agriculture, plantation, real estate or print media) or into a company or LLP that the NRI owns. A company or LLP is usually better if the NRI wants limited liability and the ability to send profits abroad. Cash can be gifted to the NRI’s NRO account; a gift from a parent is not taxed in the child’s hands.

Typical steps for route 2 (company owned by the NRI)

  1. Check the sector: FDI route and any cap, and whether the business has licences that must be re-issued.
  2. Value the business (going concern) and agree the price with the parent.
  3. Incorporate the company; the NRI subscribes for shares from abroad or from an NRE/FCNR account; report the inflow in Form FC-GPR.
  4. Sign a business transfer agreement: assets, stock, contracts, employees and liabilities move to the company.
  5. GST: the transfer of a going concern is exempt; the company takes its own GST registration, and unused input tax credit moves across through Form ITC-02.
  6. Close the parent’s proprietorship registrations once customers, suppliers and bank arrangements have moved.

Questions to settle first

  • Who will run the business day to day in India?
  • Will profits stay in India, or be sent abroad? (This usually decides between route 1 and routes 2 to 4.)
  • Are there loans, GST dues or disputes in the proprietorship?
  • Is the NRI an Indian citizen or an OCI, and how many days a year do they spend in India? That decides their residential status.

Frequently asked questions

Can an NRI take over a sole proprietorship in India?
A proprietorship can't be transferred as an entity because it is the owner personally. An NRI can start their own proprietorship and take over the business assets, but only on a non-repatriation basis and not in agriculture, plantation, real estate business or print media. Many NRIs instead set up a company or LLP that buys the business.
Is a gift from a parent in India taxable for an NRI child?
No. Gifts from relatives, including parents, are not taxable in the recipient's hands. The parent can credit a rupee gift to the NRI's NRO account within the LRS limit, and the NRI can send up to USD 1 million a financial year abroad from NRO with Forms 145 and 146.
Can a proprietorship be converted into a company tax-free?
Yes, if all the business assets and liabilities move to the company, the proprietor receives only shares, and the proprietor keeps at least 50% of the voting power for five years. Transferring those shares to a child sooner can lose the exemption.
Does GST apply when a business is transferred to a family member's company?
The transfer of a business as a going concern is exempt from GST. The new owner needs its own GST registration, and unused input tax credit can be transferred through Form ITC-02.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
