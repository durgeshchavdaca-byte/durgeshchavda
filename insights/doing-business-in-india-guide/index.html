Doing business in India: the 2026 guide for foreign companies

Singapore and the US were India’s two largest sources of foreign direct investment in 2025-26. Here’s how foreign companies enter, how they’re taxed and what they must file — updated for the new Income-tax Act and the 2026 FDI changes.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 14 October 2026·7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Most sectors allow 100% foreign ownership under the automatic route; only reporting to RBI is needed.
  • Since Press Note 2 (2026 Series), land-border-country investors with non-controlling beneficial ownership of up to 10% can use the automatic route.
  • An Indian subsidiary can opt for a 22% base tax rate (about 25.17% all-in); a branch pays 35% plus surcharge and cess.

Foreign investment rules

  • Most sectors allow 100% FDI under the automatic route — no prior approval, just reporting (FC-GPR within 30 days of issuing shares).
  • Some sectors have caps or need government approval.
  • Land-border countries (including China): since Press Note 2 (2026 Series), investors with non-controlling beneficial ownership of up to 10% can use the automatic route; above that, or with control, government approval is still needed.

Tax in brief

  • India’s Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026; the tax year runs April–March.
  • Subsidiary: 22% base rate option (about 25.17% all-in). Branch: 35% plus surcharge and cess.
  • Payments abroad (dividends, royalties, technical fees, interest) carry withholding, reduced by treaties — see repatriating profits.
  • Transfer pricing applies to every intercompany charge; GST at 18% on most services, with exports zero-rated.

Hiring

India’s four Labour Codes have applied since 21 November 2025. Payroll includes provident fund, gratuity and professional tax; contractors who work like employees create both labour and permanent establishment risk.

Annual compliance

Audit, AGM, MCA filings, income-tax and transfer-pricing returns, the FLA return to RBI by 15 July, and monthly GST, TDS and payroll filings. What that looks like in practice: Indian subsidiary compliance.

Checking partners and targets

India’s public registries are unusually open — private companies’ accounts are on file with the government. Start with how to verify an Indian company; for investments use a due diligence checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company own 100% of an Indian company?
Yes, in most sectors under the automatic route, with no prior approval — only post-investment reporting to RBI. Some sectors have caps or need government approval, as do investors with more than 10% beneficial ownership (or control) from countries sharing a land border with India.
What is the easiest way for a foreign company to start in India?
Many start with an employer of record for the first few hires, then set up a private limited subsidiary once the team or revenue grows. A liaison office suits companies that only need representation and market research.
How is a foreign company taxed in India?
An Indian subsidiary is taxed as an Indian company (22% base rate option, about 25.17% all-in). A branch is taxed at 35% plus surcharge and cess. Payments to the foreign parent carry withholding tax reduced by treaties.
How long does it take to set up a company in India?
Incorporation itself typically takes a few weeks once documents are apostilled or notarised; opening the bank account, bringing in capital and filing FC-GPR follow. Plan for one to two months end to end.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
