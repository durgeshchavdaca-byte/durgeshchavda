Living in Germany? Stop paying 30% in India on income Germany taxes anyway

Indians working in Munich, Berlin, Frankfurt, Stuttgart and Hamburg often keep NRO deposits, mutual funds, shares or a flat in India. Germany taxes that income too, and gives credit for only part of the Indian tax. The rest is lost unless you reclaim it in India. Here is how both sides work.

Key takeaways
  • Living in Germany and in India under 182 days a year: usually non-resident, so India taxes only Indian income.
  • Treaty cap on Indian interest and dividends: 10%, and Germany credits only that 10%.
  • Indian banks deduct about 30% on NRO interest: reclaim the excess through an Indian return.
  • Indian mutual funds are taxed as foreign funds in Germany (Anlage KAP, Vorabpauschale).
Quick answer: if you live in Germany and spend fewer than 182 days a year in India, you are usually a non-resident in India, so India taxes only your Indian income (NRO interest, dividends, rent, gains on Indian shares, funds and property). Germany taxes the same income because you are resident there. Under the India-Germany treaty, Indian tax on interest and dividends is capped at 10%, and Germany credits only that 10%. Indian banks usually deduct about 30% on NRO interest, so the extra 20% is lost unless you claim it back through an Indian return. Missed years: FY 2025-26 can be filed until 31 December 2026. How Germany taxes your Indian income

  • With a home or habitual abode in Germany (in practice, more than six months), you are taxed in Germany on worldwide income, including Indian interest, dividends and fund income.
  • Capital income is taxed at a flat 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (and church tax if applicable), after the saver’s allowance of €1,000 (€2,000 for couples filing jointly). Foreign capital income goes in Anlage KAP of your German return.
  • Indian tax is credited only up to the treaty rate. Tax deducted in India above that is not a German credit: it must be reclaimed in India.
  • Indian mutual funds count as foreign investment funds, so expect German tax on distributions, on the yearly advance lump sum and on gains when you sell. Many Indians in Germany simplify by moving to direct shares or deposits; plan the Indian side of any sale first.

Moving back from Germany?

In the first years after returning you may be RNOR, and income earned abroad stays outside Indian tax. Once fully resident, German accounts, shares, pension rights (including Riester or company pensions) must go in Schedule FA. Missed it in a resident year? Frequently asked questions

Do I pay tax in both India and Germany on NRO interest?
Yes, but with relief. India can tax it at up to 10% under the treaty, and Germany taxes it as capital income with credit for that 10%. Indian banks usually deduct about 30%, so the difference has to be claimed back through an Indian return.
What is the India-Germany DTAA rate on interest and dividends?
Indian tax on interest and dividends paid to a resident of Germany is capped at 10% of the gross amount. Give the payer your German tax residence certificate and Form 10F, or claim the excess in your Indian return.
Is NRE interest tax-free if I live in Germany?
It is tax-free in India for a non-resident, but Germany taxes your worldwide income, so NRE interest is still declared as capital income in your German return.
How are Indian mutual funds taxed in Germany?
They are foreign investment funds under German law, so distributions, the yearly advance lump sum (Vorabpauschale) and gains on sale are taxed in Germany. India also taxes the gain on sale for a non-resident. Plan both sides before redeeming.
I have not filed Indian returns since moving to Germany. What now?
File FY 2025-26 as a belated return by 31 December 2026 to recover excess TDS. Earlier years back to FY 2021-22 can only add income through an updated return, which cannot claim refunds.
