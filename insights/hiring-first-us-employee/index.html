Hiring your first US employee as an Indian startup

Your first US hire — usually sales or customer success — turns your Delaware company into a real US employer. That brings payroll taxes, state registrations and paperwork you can’t skip. Here’s the playbook.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·8 min read

A PEO (professional employer organisation) runs payroll, tax filings and benefits for your US company. An EOR (employer of record) employs the person on your behalf — useful if you don’t yet have a US company.

If you employ directly: set-up steps

  1. EIN for the US company — EIN guide.
  2. Register in the employee’s state: state income-tax withholding and state unemployment insurance accounts. An employee working in a state usually also means registering the company to do business there — see foreign qualification.
  3. Workers’ compensation insurance — required in most states once you have employees.
  4. Payroll provider to calculate withholding, pay taxes and file returns.
  5. Employment documents: offer letter, confidentiality and IP assignment agreement, and a handbook as you grow.
An employee in a state also usually creates nexus there for state income or franchise tax and sales tax — factor this into where you hire.

On top of these come state unemployment insurance, state (and sometimes local) income-tax withholding, and any state-specific programmes such as disability or paid family leave.

Paperwork for each new hire

  • Form I-9 — you must verify each employee’s identity and authorisation to work in the US within the first days of employment. Some states also require E-Verify.
  • Form W-4 (federal) and the state withholding form.
  • New-hire reporting to the state within its deadline.
  • Required state and federal labour-law notices.
Founders take note: the US company can’t simply put an India-based founder on US payroll to work in the US. Working in the US requires an appropriate visa or work authorisation; the I-9 check applies to founders too.

Recurring payroll filings

  • Form 941 — quarterly federal payroll return.
  • Form 940 — annual FUTA return, due 31 January.
  • Form W-2 — to each employee and the Social Security Administration by 31 January.
  • State withholding and unemployment returns on each state’s schedule.

All of these belong on your US compliance calendar.

Employee or contractor?

Paying a US-based person as an independent contractor (with a Form 1099-NEC) is simpler — but only if they genuinely are one. Control over how and when they work, integration into your team and ongoing, full-time work point towards employment. Misclassification can bring back payroll taxes, penalties and state claims, and some states apply stricter tests than the IRS.

Knock-on effects in India

  • Transfer pricing: if the US company’s team supports the Indian parent (or vice versa), the intercompany charge must be at arm’s length. See US subsidiary of an Indian company.
  • Funding payroll from India: money sent from the Indian parent is overseas investment or a loan under FEMA and must be reported. See ODI rules.
  • Stock options for US hires are granted by the US company; for the team in India, see US parent ESOPs.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a US company to hire an employee in the US?
Not necessarily. An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ the person on your behalf without a US entity. To run your own payroll or use a PEO, you need a US company with an EIN.
What payroll taxes does a US employer pay in 2026?
Employers pay 6.2% Social Security tax on wages up to $184,500, 1.45% Medicare tax on all wages, federal unemployment tax (6% on the first $7,000, usually reduced to 0.6% after credit), plus state unemployment insurance and any state programmes.
What forms are needed when hiring a US employee?
Form I-9 to verify work authorisation, Form W-4 and the state withholding form, and state new-hire reporting. Recurring filings include Form 941 quarterly, Form 940 annually and Form W-2 by 31 January.
Does hiring an employee in a US state create tax obligations there?
Usually yes. An employee working in a state generally requires payroll registrations and often registration to do business there, and can create nexus for state income, franchise and sales taxes.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
