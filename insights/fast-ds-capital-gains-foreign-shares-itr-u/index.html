FAST-DS · Capital gains · Nov 2026

Sold foreign shares and never reported the gain? FAST-DS 60% or ITR-U 12.5%?

This is the question we hear most from people with US or other foreign shares: the gain would be taxed at only 12.5% in an updated return, but FAST-DS charges 60%. So why not just file ITR-U? Because an updated return does not count under the Black Money Act. Here is how the two really compare, in plain words, and the case where neither is the best answer.

Key takeaways
  • FAST-DS charges 60% of an unreported foreign gain (Category A); shares still held from taxed salary cost a flat ₹1 lakh (Category B).
  • ITR-U at 12.5% does not count under the Black Money Act: 120% and the ₹10 lakh penalty remain possible.
  • Sold in FY 2025-26? A revised return by 31 March 2027 settles both laws at the normal rate.
Quick answer: if you were resident in India when you sold foreign shares and the gain was never reported, FAST-DS treats the gain as undisclosed foreign income and charges 60% of the gain (Category A). An updated return (ITR-U) charges the normal 12.5% plus interest and 25% to 70% extra, but it does not protect you under the Black Money Act: that Act treats income as undisclosed unless it was shown in an on-time, belated or revised return, and an updated return is none of these. If the sale was in FY 2025-26, a revised return by 31 March 2027 at the normal rate settles it, under the Income-tax Act and the Black Money Act. If you still hold the shares and bought them from taxed salary, there is no gain to declare: they go in Category B for a flat ₹1 lakh.

Why FAST-DS charges 60% on the gain

  • FAST-DS Category A covers an undisclosed foreign asset or undisclosed foreign income, meaning foreign income that was taxable in India but not offered to tax.
  • A capital gain on foreign shares sold while you were resident is exactly that. The amount payable is 30% of the income declared plus an equal amount: 60%.
  • No foreign tax credit is allowed inside the scheme; any treaty credit belongs only in the return for that year.
  • Shares you still hold are different. If they were bought from salary already taxed in India (including RSUs and ESPP taxed through payroll), they are an asset “already offered to tax”: Category B, one flat ₹1 lakh for all such assets and years, if their total is within ₹5 crore. Unrealised gains are not taxed.

Only foreign bank accounts? Small balances may need no declaration at all, and the value is the total of deposits, not the balance: FAST-DS for foreign bank accounts.

US shares bought through Vested, INDmoney or a US broker while living in India? FAST-DS for US stocks held through Indian apps.

Missed income in an older year? An updated return (ITR-U) is possible up to 48 months later; the window for FY 2021-22 closes on 31 March 2027: ITR-U time limit and extra tax by year.

Why ITR-U at 12.5% is not the safe shortcut

  • The Black Money Act counts foreign income as undisclosed if it was not shown in a return filed on time, belated or revised (sections 139(1), 139(4) and 139(5)). An updated return is filed under section 139(8A), which is not on that list.
  • So after an ITR-U you have paid the Income-tax Act cost, but the gain can still be taxed under the Black Money Act at 30% plus a 90% penalty (120%), and missing the shares in Schedule FA can still attract a ₹10 lakh penalty. These are the figures the official FAQs give for outside the scheme.
  • An updated return also cannot be used to reduce tax, and it carries 25% to 70% additional tax on top of tax and interest, depending on how late it is.

The year you came back to India: are you resident?

Many people with foreign shares had returned to India part-way through a year. Whether that year counts as a resident year decides whether foreign gains are taxable and whether the shares had to be in Schedule FA. Practitioners often get this wrong.

  • You are resident in a year if you spend 182 days or more in India, or 60 days or more in that year and 365 days or more in the previous four years.
  • The relaxation that replaces 60 days with 182 for someone who leaves India for employment applies only to the year of leaving.
  • The relaxation for an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin who comes on a visit applies to visits. Coming back for good is not a visit, so the normal 60-day test applies in the year of return.
ExampleAnswer
In India until April 2022, left for a job abroad on 25 April 2022, came back for good on 15 January 2025FY 2024-25: about 76 days in India (15 January to 31 March 2025). That is 60 days or more, and he spent far more than 365 days in India in FY 2020-21 to 2023-24. So he is resident for FY 2024-25.
Is he “not ordinarily resident” (RNOR)?No. RNOR needs non-residence in 9 of the previous 10 years, or 729 days or less in India in the previous 7 years. Neither applies, so he is resident and ordinarily resident.
What followsHis foreign income for the whole of FY 2024-25 (including salary abroad from April 2024 to January 2025, and any foreign share gains) is taxable in India, with foreign tax credit through Form 67 and any relief the tax treaty gives. His foreign assets had to be in Schedule FA for that year. The time for a belated or revised FY 2024-25 return ended on 31 December 2025, so a missed foreign asset for that year is a FAST-DS case.

Other rules that decide the numbers

  • Long-term or short-term: foreign shares are long-term only after 24 months. Short-term gains are taxed at your slab rate.
  • Old sales: long-term gains on sales before 23 July 2024 were taxed at 20% with indexation, not 12.5%.
  • One Form 1: both categories can go in a single Form 1, and only one counts; it can be refiled only until Form 2 is issued.
  • Late filing of returns: a missed return attracts a late fee of ₹1,000 or ₹5,000 and interest, separately from all of the above.

Frequently asked questions

Is long-term capital gain on foreign shares taxed at 60% under FAST-DS?
If the gain arose while you were resident and was never offered to tax, FAST-DS treats it as undisclosed foreign income in Category A: 30% tax plus an equal amount, so 60% of the gain. Shares you still hold, bought from taxed salary, go in Category B for a flat ₹1 lakh instead.
Can I report the foreign share gain in ITR-U at 12.5% instead of FAST-DS?
You can pay the Income-tax Act cost that way, with interest and 25% to 70% additional tax. But the Black Money Act treats income as undisclosed unless it was shown in an on-time, belated or revised return, and an updated return is none of these, so the Black Money Act exposure remains.
Does ITR-U give immunity from the Black Money Act?
No. The Black Money Act refers to returns filed under sections 139(1), 139(4) and 139(5) of the Income-tax Act. An updated return is filed under section 139(8A), which is not covered.
What if I sold the foreign shares in FY 2025-26?
Then file a revised return for FY 2025-26 by 31 March 2027 showing the gain and the foreign assets. The official FAST-DS FAQs say this is treated as sufficient compliance under the Black Money Act, and the gain is taxed at the normal rate.
I came back to India for good part-way through the year. Am I resident for that year?
Usually yes, if you spent 60 days or more in India that year and 365 days or more in the previous four years. The 182-day relaxation for people leaving for employment applies only to the year of leaving, and the relaxation for visits does not cover a permanent return. Whether you are also not ordinarily resident depends on the 9-of-10-years and 729-days tests.
What is the penalty under the Black Money Act if I do nothing?
According to the official FAQs, undisclosed foreign income or assets are charged 30% tax plus a 90% penalty, 120% in total, and assets taxed but not shown in Schedule FA attract a ₹10 lakh penalty for each asset. Prosecution provisions also exist for wilful cases.
