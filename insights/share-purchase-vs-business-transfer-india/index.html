Acquiring an Indian business? Buy the shares, the business, or merge

Foreign buyers can acquire an Indian business in three main ways. Each moves different risks to you and is taxed and documented differently. Choosing the structure early saves months, because it decides which approvals, valuations and filings you need.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 15 October 2026·6 min read
Choose the right deal structure hello@durgeshchavda.com
Key takeaways
  • Share purchase: simplest, but you inherit all past liabilities.
  • Business transfer: cleaner, but needs an Indian buyer entity and moves licences and staff.
  • Merger: tribunal scheme, longest timeline.
Quick answer: a share purchase is simplest and keeps licences and contracts in place, but you inherit every past liability, so due diligence and indemnities matter most. A business transfer (slump sale) lets you pick the business and leave liabilities behind, but you need an Indian company to buy it, and licences, contracts and employees must move across. A merger needs a tribunal-approved scheme and takes longest. For share purchases from Indian residents, FEMA pricing rules apply and the transfer is reported in Form FC-TRS.

How to choose

  • Clean company, regulated licences: share purchase with strong warranties, indemnities and escrow.
  • Messy history, attractive business: business transfer into your own Indian subsidiary.
  • Group restructuring or combining with your existing Indian entity: merger.
  • Sensitive sectors or a buyer from a country sharing a land border with India: government approval may be needed first.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company buy the business of an Indian company directly?
Not usually as a business transfer. The business and its assets are normally bought by an Indian company, typically the foreign buyer's subsidiary, which is funded through FDI. A foreign buyer can, however, buy shares of the Indian company directly.
What FEMA rules apply when a foreign buyer purchases shares from Indian residents?
The price must not be lower than the fair value set under the pricing guidelines, sector caps and approval routes apply, and the transfer must be reported in Form FC-TRS.
Is a business transfer subject to GST in India?
A transfer of a business as a going concern is exempt from GST, but stamp duty applies to the transfer deed and to certain assets under state law.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
