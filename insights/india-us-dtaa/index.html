India–US DTAA: treaty rates and how to actually claim them

Money moving between India and the US — dividends from your US company, fees from US clients, royalties to a US parent — is taxed in both countries unless you use the treaty correctly.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·8 min read
Get treaty advice hello@durgeshchavda.com

What the India–US DTAA does

The India–US Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (the 1989 Convention) decides which country may tax each type of cross-border income, caps the tax the source country can withhold, and requires the residence country to give credit for tax already paid. It is the reason a dividend from your Delaware company, or a service fee from a US client, doesn’t have to be taxed twice in full.

Treaty rates at a glance

Maximum tax the source country can withhold under the treaty (domestic law may be lower; where the treaty rate is higher than domestic law, you pay the lower):

IncomeTreaty articleMaximum source-country rate
Dividends — company holding at least 10% of voting stockArticle 1015%
Dividends — all other casesArticle 1025%
Interest — loans from banks / financial institutionsArticle 1110%
Interest — other casesArticle 1115%
Royalties for use of industrial, commercial or scientific equipment, and related servicesArticle 1210%
Other royalties and fees for included servicesArticle 1215%
Business profits without a permanent establishment in the other countryArticle 7Not taxable in the source country

Fees for services — the tricky one

The India–US treaty doesn’t tax every technical fee at source. “Fees for included services” covers services that are ancillary to a royalty, or that make available technical knowledge, skills, know-how or processes to the payer so they can use it themselves. Routine services that don’t transfer that capability — many consulting, marketing or support services — may instead be business profits, taxable in the source country only if there’s a permanent establishment. Getting this characterisation right is often the difference between 15% withholding and none.

Claiming treaty rates

When a US payer pays an Indian person or company

  • Give the US payer Form W-8BEN-E (entities) or W-8BEN (individuals), claiming treaty benefits under the relevant article — see how to fill W-8BEN-E.
  • Without it, US payers generally withhold at the full 30% statutory rate on US-source fixed income such as dividends or royalties.

When an Indian payer pays a US person or company

  • The US recipient provides a Tax Residency Certificate (for US residents, typically IRS Form 6166) and the self-declaration India requires (Form 10F, filed electronically).
  • The Indian payer applies the lower of the domestic or treaty rate, and completes the remittance documentation with its bank and chartered accountant.

Foreign tax credit in India

An Indian resident taxed in the US on income that India also taxes can claim a foreign tax credit in India, limited to the Indian tax on that income. The claim requires a prescribed statement, filed before or with the Indian return:

  • Form 67 for foreign income up to FY 2025-26.
  • Form 44 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 for income from 1 April 2026 onwards.

Keep proof of US tax paid — US returns, withholding statements or Form 1042-S.

2026: India’s new Income-tax Act

From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 and Income-tax Rules, 2026 replaced the 1961 Act, and many forms were renumbered. For cross-border payments this matters in practice: remittance documentation formerly done in Forms 15CA/15CB now uses Forms 145/146 for payments on or after 1 April 2026, and the transfer-pricing accountant’s report formerly in Form 3CEB is now Form 48. The India–US treaty itself is unchanged.

Founder scenarios

  • Your Delaware C-Corp pays you a dividend: US withholding applies (25% for an individual shareholder under the treaty, if you give a W-8BEN); you pay Indian tax on the dividend and claim credit for the US tax. See C-Corp vs LLC.
  • Your Indian company sells software services to US clients: usually business profits taxable only in India if you have no US permanent establishment — give clients a W-8BEN-E.
  • Your US subsidiary pays the Indian parent for development work: priced at arm’s length and documented for transfer pricing. See US subsidiary of an Indian company.
  • You own a US LLC: the US may ignore the LLC while India treats it as a separate company, which can complicate treaty access and credits. See ODI & tax rules for US companies.

Frequently asked questions

What is the dividend tax rate under the India–US DTAA?
The source country can tax dividends at up to 15% when the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the payer's voting stock, and up to 25% in other cases.
How do I claim India–US treaty rates on payments from US clients?
Provide the US payer with Form W-8BEN-E (for a company) or W-8BEN (for an individual) claiming the relevant treaty article. Without it, US payers generally withhold at 30% on fixed income such as dividends and royalties.
Which form is used to claim foreign tax credit in India?
Form 67 for foreign income up to FY 2025-26, and Form 44 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 for income from 1 April 2026. It must be filed before or along with the income-tax return.
Did India's new Income-tax Act change the India–US DTAA?
No. The treaty is unchanged, but from 1 April 2026 Indian domestic forms were renumbered — for example Forms 145/146 replace 15CA/15CB and Form 48 replaces Form 3CEB.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
