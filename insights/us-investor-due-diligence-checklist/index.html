The US investor due diligence checklist for India-run startups

US investors run the same diligence on every Delaware company — and a few extra checks when the founders and team are in India. Most issues are fixable in weeks if you find them first. Here’s the list to work through before the term sheet.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·8 min read
Get a pre-diligence review hello@durgeshchavda.com

1. Corporate records (Delaware)

  • Certificate of incorporation and every amendment; bylaws.
  • Board and stockholder consents for every share issuance, option grant, SAFE and major contract.
  • A current certificate of good standing — which requires franchise tax and annual reports to be up to date.
  • Registered agent in place and foreign qualifications in states where you have employees or offices.

2. Equity and cap table

  • A cap table that reconciles to board approvals, stock ledger and share certificates.
  • Founder stock purchase agreements with vesting, and proof of timely 83(b) elections where relevant.
  • All SAFEs and convertible notes, with a conversion model — see SAFE notes.
  • An option plan approved by the board and stockholders, grants priced on a current 409A valuation.
  • If the company converted from an LLC, the conversion documents — see LLC to C-Corp.

3. US tax and state compliance

  • Filed Form 1120 for every year since incorporation — including zero-revenue years.
  • Form 5472 for each year with foreign related-party transactions (founder capital counts).
  • Delaware franchise tax paid on the right method — see the franchise tax guide.
  • Forms 1099 and 1042-S where you paid contractors or foreign persons; payroll filings if you have US employees.
  • A sales-tax nexus review if you sell into US states.

4. India-side compliance (the extra checks)

  • Founders’ ODI: each India-resident founder’s investment in the Delaware company reported in Form FC through the AD bank, with Annual Performance Reports filed every year — see ODI rules.
  • Indian subsidiary: FDI from the Delaware parent reported via FC-GPR (and FC-TRS for any transfers), annual FLA returns filed, and ROC compliance up to date.
  • If you flipped: the share swap, valuation and ODI filings behind the flip, and the tax treatment in India.
  • Transfer pricing: an intercompany agreement and arm’s-length pricing between the US parent and Indian subsidiary, with annual documentation — see transfer pricing.
  • ESOPs for the India team: Form OPI filings and TDS on exercises — see US parent ESOPs.
  • POEM: evidence of where the US company’s key management decisions are made.

5. IP and contracts

  • IP assignment agreements from every founder, employee and contractor — in both companies — assigning work to the right entity.
  • If development happens in India, an intercompany agreement confirming that IP created by the Indian subsidiary belongs to (or is licensed to) the parent, as intended.
  • Trademark filings in the right owner’s name — see US trademark.
  • Key customer, vendor and financing contracts; any change-of-control clauses.

6. Financials

  • Monthly accrual-basis financials for both entities, and consolidated figures — see US bookkeeping.
  • Intercompany balances that reconcile between the US and Indian books.
  • Bank statements, revenue support (contracts, invoices, deferred revenue) and a current financial model.

Building the data room

  1. Organise folders by the six sections above, with a separate India folder.
  2. Include a one-page compliance summary: every filing, its date and its status — use the compliance calendar as the template.
  3. Disclose known gaps with a remediation plan; late FEMA filings can often be regularised and US filings corrected before closing.

Frequently asked questions

What do US investors check in a Delaware startup run from India?
The standard items — corporate records, cap table, 83(b) elections, 409A valuations, Form 1120 and Form 5472 filings, franchise tax and good standing, IP assignments and financials — plus India-specific items such as founders' ODI filings, FC-GPR for the Indian subsidiary, transfer pricing and ESOP reporting.
Why do investors ask about Form 5472?
Missing Form 5472 filings for a foreign-owned US company carry $25,000 penalties per form, which becomes a liability of the company the investors are buying into.
Can late FEMA filings be fixed before a funding round?
Often yes — late reporting can usually be regularised by paying a late submission fee, or through compounding for longer delays. Doing this before diligence avoids it becoming a closing condition.
What goes in a startup data room?
Corporate and legal documents, the cap table and equity documents, tax and compliance filings (US and India), IP and key contracts, financial statements and the financial model, organised by section with a compliance summary.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
