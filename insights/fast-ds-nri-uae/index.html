Indian in the UAE or back from the Gulf? Check FAST-DS before 31 December

The income tax department now receives details of Indians’ foreign bank accounts and investments every year, and has been emailing people whose returns do not show them. If you live in the UAE and the Gulf, or lived there and have moved back, the question is simple: were you resident in India in any year when you held those accounts? If yes, FAST-DS 2026 is a one-time way to fix it at a fixed cost, and it closes on 31 December 2026.

Key takeaways
  • FAST-DS applies only for years you were resident in India while holding foreign assets.
  • Flat Rs 1 lakh where the asset came from taxed or non-resident income.
  • File online from abroad by 31 December 2026.
Quick answer: FAST-DS is not for everyone who lives in the UAE and the Gulf. It applies if you were resident in India in a year when you held a foreign account or asset and did not report it: usually the year you moved, years before you moved, or years after you returned. Where the asset came from money already taxed or earned while you were non-resident, the cost is a flat ₹1 lakh. The window closes on 31 December 2026. If you were non-resident in every year you held the asset, you have nothing to declare.

Updated 22 November 2026: what the Department’s new FAQs settled. The Supplementary FAQs on FAST-DS (questions 51 to 93, released on 20 November) confirm that:

  • Category B is one flat fee of ₹1 lakh for all your assets and all the years, as long as their total in your Form 1 is within ₹5 crore (Q82, Q83).
  • An asset bought even partly from income that was not taxed cannot go in Category B. It can be declared in Category A, at 60%, if eligible (Q70).
  • US dollars convert at ₹94.6543, the RBI reference rate of 30 March 2026, as none was published on 31 March (Q55).
  • On a joint account, each holder whose name is on it declares it, at his share, or an equal share if none is fixed (Q57, Q58).
  • If FY 2025-26 is the only year in which it was missed, a revised return for that year filed by 31 March 2027 is treated as enough (Q68).
  • Only one Form 1 counts. It can be refiled until Form 2 is issued. After that, nothing can be added (Q84).

What usually gets missed in the UAE and the Gulf

  • UAE and Gulf bank accounts and fixed deposits kept open after returning
  • End-of-service benefits and workplace savings plans
  • Shares in a free-zone or mainland company
  • Property in Dubai or elsewhere in the Gulf
  • National Bonds, gold and investment accounts
  • Accounts held jointly with family members who live in India

Who this is for: Indians working or running businesses in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait and Bahrain, and families who have returned to India.

How to file from the UAE and the Gulf

  1. List your years: days in India for each financial year, from passport stamps.
  2. Collect statements from the date each account was opened. Bank accounts are valued at the total of all deposits, not the balance, so old statements matter.
  3. Value each asset as on 31 March 2026 and convert at the reference rate for that date.
  4. File Form 1 online on the Indian e-filing portal with your PAN, verified by Aadhaar OTP or a digital signature. No visit to India is needed.
  5. Pay after the department issues Form 2, then file Form 3. Form 1 must be in by 31 December 2026.

Points specific to the UAE and the Gulf

  • Returned to India? After two or three “not ordinarily resident” years, every Gulf account, company and property must be reported each year. This is the most common FAST-DS case for Gulf families.
  • Deemed residency: the UAE has no personal income tax, so an Indian citizen with Indian income above ₹15 lakh can be treated as resident in India, but as “not ordinarily resident”, which does not require foreign asset reporting.
  • NRO interest: with a UAE tax residency certificate the India–UAE treaty rate of 12.5% can apply instead of about 30%.

Other India tax work NRIs in the UAE and the Gulf ask us for

  • Refunds: filing the Indian return to get back excess TDS on rent, NRO interest and property sales. The last date for FY 2025-26 is 31 December 2026.
  • Selling property in India: a lower-TDS certificate before the sale, the capital gains computation and repatriation.
  • Moving money out of India: up to USD 1 million a year from the NRO account, with Forms 15CA and 15CB.
  • Bank and investment status: converting resident accounts to NRO or NRE and updating KYC.
  • Planning a return to India: timing the move and using the “not ordinarily resident” years well.

Frequently asked questions

Do NRIs in the UAE and the Gulf need to file FAST-DS?
Only if they were resident in India in a year when they held a foreign account or asset and did not report it, for example the year of moving, earlier years in India, or years after returning. People who were non-resident in every year they held the asset do not need FAST-DS.
Can I file FAST-DS from the UAE and the Gulf?
Yes. Form 1 is filed online on the Indian e-filing portal with your PAN, verified by Aadhaar OTP or a digital signature, and payment is made online after the department issues Form 2.
What does FAST-DS cost?
A flat Rs 1 lakh where the asset was bought from income already taxed in India or earned while non-resident and only the reporting was missed, for assets up to Rs 5 crore. For foreign income that escaped Indian tax, it is 60% (30% tax plus an equal amount), up to Rs 1 crore.
What is the last date for FAST-DS 2026?
31 December 2026. No declaration can be filed after that date.
I returned to India from Dubai and still have my UAE bank account. Do I need FAST-DS?
If you have become resident and ordinarily resident in India and the UAE account was not reported in Schedule FA, yes, FAST-DS can regularise it, usually at a flat Rs 1 lakh where the money was earned while you were non-resident. Years in which you were still not ordinarily resident did not need reporting.
