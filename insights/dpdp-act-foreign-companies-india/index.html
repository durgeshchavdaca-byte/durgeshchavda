Indian customers, Indian vendors? India’s data protection law, explained for foreign companies

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) and the DPDP Rules, 2025 are being switched on in stages. The consent-manager framework became operative on 13 November 2026 and the main obligations for businesses apply from 13 May 2027. If you sell to people in India, run an app used in India, or send data to an Indian outsourcing partner, here is what matters and what to do in the next six months.

Key takeaways
  • DPDP applies to foreign firms offering goods or services to people in India.
  • Main obligations start 13 May 2027; breach detail report within 72 hours.
  • Outsourced processing of non-Indian data is mostly exempt (s.17(1)(d)).
Quick answer: the DPDP Act applies to a foreign company if it processes personal data outside India in connection with offering goods or services to people in India (section 3). The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025; the consent-manager rule started on 13 November 2026; notice, consent, security, breach reporting, children’s data and data-subject rights apply from 13 May 2027. Penalties run up to ₹250 crore per breach. If you only send non-Indian customer data to an Indian vendor under contract, most of the Act does not apply to that processing, but security duties still do.

Your main duties from 13 May 2027

  • Notice and consent: a clear, stand-alone notice listing the personal data, the purpose and what it enables; consent must be free, specific, informed and by clear affirmative action, and withdrawing it must be as easy as giving it.
  • Security safeguards: encryption or similar measures, access control, monitoring, and keeping logs and related data for at least one year.
  • Breach reporting: tell affected users and the Data Protection Board without delay, and send the Board a detailed report within 72 hours. CERT-In’s separate six-hour reporting rule for cyber incidents still applies.
  • Children: verifiable parental consent before processing data of anyone under 18, with limited exemptions.
  • Processors: you stay responsible for what your vendors do; use a written contract.
  • Rights and grievances: users can access, correct and erase data and nominate someone; publish how, and answer within the set time.
  • Erasure for large platforms: e-commerce and social media platforms with over 2 crore Indian users and online gaming platforms with over 50 lakh must erase data after three years of inactivity, with 48 hours’ notice.
  • Significant Data Fiduciaries (if the government notifies you): an India-based Data Protection Officer, an independent data auditor, and an annual impact assessment and audit.

Can data leave India?

Yes. Transfers outside India are allowed unless the government restricts a country by notification, and the Rules let it set conditions by order. Significant Data Fiduciaries may have to keep government-specified categories of data in India. Sector rules (RBI payment data, insurance, telecom) can be stricter, so check them separately.

A 6-step plan for the next six months

  1. Map Indian data: which products, apps, entities and vendors touch personal data of people in India.
  2. Decide who is the fiduciary: the foreign parent, the Indian subsidiary, or both, and document it.
  3. Fix notices and consent flows for Indian users, including withdrawal and children.
  4. Paper your vendors: data processing clauses with Indian and foreign processors, security standards and breach timelines.
  5. Build the breach playbook: who decides, who notifies the Board, CERT-In and users, and the 72-hour report.
  6. Check your Indian partners: before sharing data, diligence the vendor’s security, ownership and track record.

Frequently asked questions

Does India's DPDP Act apply to foreign companies?
Yes, if the foreign company processes digital personal data outside India in connection with offering goods or services to people in India (section 3(b) of the DPDP Act, 2023). An Indian entity is not needed for the Act to apply.
When do the DPDP Rules come into force?
The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025. The Data Protection Board provisions applied immediately, the consent-manager rule from 13 November 2026, and the main business obligations from 13 May 2027.
Does the DPDP Act apply when a foreign company outsources work to India?
If an Indian vendor processes personal data of people outside India under a contract with a foreign company, section 17(1)(d) exempts that processing from most of the Act. The vendor must still take reasonable security safeguards, and the cross-border transfer rules still apply.
How fast must a data breach be reported under the DPDP Rules?
The data fiduciary must inform affected users and the Data Protection Board without delay, and send the Board a detailed report within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach. CERT-In's separate six-hour cyber incident reporting rule also applies.
What is the maximum penalty under the DPDP Act?
Up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards. Failing to report a breach or breaching children's data obligations can cost up to Rs 200 crore each.
