Indian subsidiary incorporated? Opening its bank account and bringing in the capital

Once your Indian company is incorporated, the bank account is the step that most often stalls. Banks run full KYC on the company, its foreign shareholders and its directors, and the first money from abroad starts two FEMA clocks. Here is the order that works, the documents banks ask for, and the deadlines you cannot miss.

Key takeaways
  • Open a current account with an AD Category-I bank after incorporation.
  • Issue shares within 60 days of receiving the money; refund otherwise.
  • File FC-GPR on FIRMS within 30 days of issuing the shares.
Quick answer: open a current account in the Indian company’s name with an authorised dealer (AD Category-I) bank, after incorporation. Banks need the company’s incorporation documents and PAN, a board resolution, and KYC of directors, signatories and every beneficial owner holding over 10%, which includes the foreign parent. The parent then remits the share capital through banking channels. The company must issue shares within 60 days of receiving the money (or refund it within the next 15 days) and file Form FC-GPR within 30 days of issuing them.

The order that works

  1. Incorporate the company with the subscriber shares shown in the memorandum. No account can be opened, and no capital received, before this.
  2. Choose the bank: an AD Category-I bank that handles foreign investment regularly. A bank where your group already banks abroad can speed up the parent’s KYC.
  3. Open the current account with the documents below.
  4. Remit the share capital from the parent’s own bank account abroad, quoting the purpose as investment in shares.
  5. Get the inward remittance certificate and KYC report from the Indian bank, based on confirmation from the remitting bank.
  6. Issue the shares within 60 days of receiving the money, and file FC-GPR on the RBI’s FIRMS portal within 30 days of issue, with a valuation certificate and the company secretary’s declaration.

Documents banks usually ask for

The Indian companyCertificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, company PAN, board resolution opening the account and naming signatories, registered office proof; often GST registration or other business proof
Foreign shareholder (parent)Incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, shareholding chart up to the ultimate owners, board resolution for the investment; usually notarised and apostilled or consularised
Beneficial owners over 10%Identity and address proof of each natural person who ultimately owns or controls more than 10%
Directors and signatoriesPassport (foreign nationals), proof of address, photograph; Indian residents give PAN and Aadhaar; in-person or video KYC as the bank requires

Where it usually goes wrong

  • Money sent before the account or company exists: it bounces back or sits unallocated.
  • Remittance from a third party instead of the shareholder itself: the bank cannot match it to the investor.
  • The 60-day window missed: shares not issued in time means refunding the money and a FEMA contravention.
  • FC-GPR filed late: late submission fees apply, and repeated delays lead to compounding.
  • No resident signatory: many banks want at least one authorised signatory available in India. Your resident director often fills this role.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company open a bank account for its Indian subsidiary before incorporation?
No. The account is opened in the Indian company's own name, so it has to be incorporated first. The share capital is remitted after the account is open.
How long does an Indian company have to issue shares after receiving foreign investment?
Under the FEMA non-debt instruments rules, the shares must be issued within 60 days of receiving the money. If they are not, the amount must be refunded within the next 15 days.
When is Form FC-GPR due?
Within 30 days of issuing the shares to the foreign investor, filed on the RBI's FIRMS portal through the company's authorised dealer bank, with a valuation certificate and the company secretary's declaration.
Do foreign directors need to visit India to open the company's bank account?
Not always. Many banks accept video KYC or documents notarised and apostilled abroad, but most want at least one authorised signatory who is available in India, often the resident director.
