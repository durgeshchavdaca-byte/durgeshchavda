Employee benefits in India: what a foreign employer must offer

Hiring your first people in India, through a subsidiary, a branch or an employer of record? Indian law sets a floor of benefits that cannot be contracted out of, and the four Labour Codes that took effect on 21 November 2025 changed how several of them are calculated. Here is the floor, and what competitive employers add on top.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 15 October 2026·6 min read

Key takeaways
  • Mandatory: PF (12%), ESI (lower earners), gratuity, statutory bonus, maternity leave, state leave.
  • PF ceiling raised to ₹25,000 a month from 17 September 2026.
  • Labour Codes: 50% wage rule, gratuity for fixed-term staff after a year, final dues in 2 working days.
Quick answer: the legal minimum is provident fund (12% employer contribution), ESI health insurance for lower earners, gratuity on leaving, a statutory bonus for lower earners, 26 weeks’ maternity leave, and paid leave under state law. To compete for talent, most foreign employers add group health insurance, term life cover and a performance bonus.

What the Labour Codes changed

  • The 50% wage rule. “Wages” means basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. If excluded allowances are more than 50% of total pay, the excess is added back. Salary structures with a low basic and high allowances now cost more in PF and gratuity.
  • Gratuity for fixed-term staff after one year of service, worked out pro rata.
  • Full and final settlement within two working days of an employee leaving.
  • Appointment letters for every employee, and a single registration and return regime as states notify their rules. Central rules came in May 2026; states are notifying theirs on a rolling basis, so check the state where your team sits.
  • PF ceiling to ₹25,000 (notification S.O. 5109(E), 17 September 2026): more mid-level employees now fall under mandatory PF.

What good employers add

  • Group health insurance covering the employee’s spouse, children and often parents. Not mandatory, but expected by almost every professional hire.
  • Term life and personal accident cover.
  • Employer NPS contribution: tax-efficient for the employee, deductible up to 14% of salary under the new tax regime.
  • Annual performance bonus and, for senior roles, ESOPs or RSUs in the foreign parent. See parent-company ESOPs for Indian employees.
  • Meal cards, internet and home-office allowances, structured within the 50% rule.

Checklist before the first hire

  1. Pick the route: EOR, subsidiary or branch.
  2. Register for PF, ESI (if applicable), professional tax and the state Shops and Establishments Act.
  3. Design the salary structure so “wages” are at least 50% of pay, and budget PF and gratuity on that base.
  4. Buy group health and gratuity insurance.
  5. Issue appointment letters and a leave policy that meets the state rules.
  6. Set up monthly payroll, TDS on salary and quarterly TDS returns.

Can a foreign company buy group medical cover (GMC) for Indian staff without an Indian entity?

Usually not directly. Indian group health policies are issued to an Indian policyholder, normally the employer. Without an Indian entity, the common routes are:

  • Through your employer of record: the EOR adds your employees to its group medical policy, or buys one for your team. This is the simplest option.
  • A global or international health plan arranged through an international broker, if your insurer can cover employees resident in India. Check that it works with Indian hospitals for cashless treatment.
  • Reimbursing individual policies the employees buy themselves, which is administratively simple but less valuable to employees and may be taxable for them.

Once you set up an Indian subsidiary, it can buy a standard Indian group policy directly. Insurance rules change, so confirm the structure with a licensed broker.

Frequently asked questions

What benefits must a foreign company give employees in India?
Provident fund (12% employer contribution, mandatory up to ₹25,000 monthly wages), ESI for employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month, gratuity on leaving, a statutory bonus for lower earners, 26 weeks' maternity leave and paid leave under state law. Group health insurance is not mandatory but is expected.
Is PF mandatory for all employees in India?
PF is mandatory for employees whose wages are up to ₹25,000 a month (the ceiling was raised from ₹15,000 on 17 September 2026) in establishments with 20 or more employees. Above the ceiling, the employer and employee can agree to contribute.
Do fixed-term employees get gratuity in India?
Yes. Under the Code on Social Security, in force since 21 November 2025, fixed-term employees qualify for gratuity after one year of service, on a pro-rata basis. Other employees need five years.
Is health insurance mandatory for employees in India?
Only ESI, the state scheme, is mandatory, and only for employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month in covered establishments. Private group health insurance is optional but almost universal for professional roles.
What is the 50% wage rule under the Labour Codes?
Wages for PF, gratuity and other benefits mean basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. If the allowances left out are more than 50% of total pay, the excess is added back to wages, which raises PF and gratuity costs.
Can a foreign company buy group medical cover in India without an Indian entity?
Usually not directly, because Indian group health policies are issued to an Indian policyholder. The common routes are the employer of record's group policy, a global health plan arranged through an international broker, or reimbursing individual policies. An Indian subsidiary can buy a group policy itself.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
