The H-1B fee fight — and the India option

Whatever happens to the $100,000 H-1B fee in court, US companies are rethinking where engineering and operations work gets done. Here’s the status, and the three ways to build a team in India.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 15 October 2026·5 min read
Plan your India team hello@durgeshchavda.com
Key takeaways
  • A US court vacated the $100,000 H-1B fee in June 2026; it isn't being collected, but the legal fight continues.
  • US companies can build in India through a vendor, an employer of record or their own subsidiary (GCC).
  • A cost-plus Indian subsidiary is the usual long-term structure.

Where the fee stands (October 2026)

  • A September 2025 proclamation imposed a $100,000 payment on new H-1B petitions for workers outside the US.
  • In June 2026 a federal court in Massachusetts held the fee unlawful and vacated it; the appeals court refused to pause that ruling in July, and the fee has not been collected since.
  • Another court upheld the fee in December 2025, and a new proclamation in September 2026 extended it to September 2027 — so the legal position is unsettled. Check with immigration counsel before relying on either outcome.

Tax and cost points

  • A subsidiary billing the US parent cost plus a markup; eligible IT and ITeS units can use the safe-harbour option under India’s Income-tax Rules, 2026.
  • Services exported to the parent can be zero-rated for GST.
  • Staff without an entity can create permanent-establishment risk — plan before hiring.

Frequently asked questions

Is the $100,000 H-1B fee still in effect?
As of October 2026 it is not being collected: a federal court vacated it in June 2026 and the appeals court refused to stay that ruling. But another court upheld it and a new proclamation extended it to 2027, so the position is unsettled — check with immigration counsel.
How can a US company build a team in India?
Through an outsourcing vendor, an employer of record for the first hires, or its own Indian subsidiary (often called a GCC) charging the parent cost plus a markup.
Is a GCC in India tax-efficient for a US company?
It is taxed in India on its markup, usually at a 22% base rate option, and services to the parent can be zero-rated for GST; transfer-pricing documentation is essential.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
