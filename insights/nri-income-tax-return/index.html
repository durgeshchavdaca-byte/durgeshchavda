The NRI income tax return: who files, and how

Most NRIs who file in India do it to get money back — excess TDS on a property sale, rent or NRO interest. Here’s what you need to know.

Do you need to file?

  • You must file if your Indian taxable income exceeds the basic exemption limit.
  • You should file whenever more TDS was deducted than you owe — common with property sales, rent and NRO interest.
  • Filing also creates a clean record for future repatriation and for your bank.

What’s taxed — and what isn’t

  • Taxed: Indian rent, NRO interest, capital gains on Indian property and shares, Indian business income.
  • Not taxed: income earned and received abroad, and NRE interest.
  • NRIs don’t report foreign assets — the foreign assets schedule applies only to residents who are ordinarily resident.
  • Not sure you’re an NRI this year? See residential status.

Which form, which date

  • ITR-2 for most NRIs (salary, rent, capital gains, other income); ITR-3 if you have business or professional income in India. NRIs can’t use ITR-1.
  • For income of 2025-26, the due date was 31 July 2026 for most individuals (31 August for non-audit business cases, and 21 November 2026 for audit cases after the CBDT extension).
  • Returns for income from 1 April 2026 onwards are filed under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Getting TDS back

  1. Check your tax credit statement (Form 168, formerly 26AS) for all TDS.
  2. Compute the actual tax — capital gains after costs, exemptions and treaty rates.
  3. File the return and verify it; the refund goes to a bank account linked to your PAN (an NRO account works).

How to file from abroad, step by step

  1. Confirm your status for the year. Count your days in India from passport stamps. Fewer than 182 days usually means non-resident; check the 120-day rule if your Indian income is above ₹15 lakh.
  2. Log in to the e-filing portal with your PAN. Update your profile to non-resident and add your overseas address and a contact number you can reach.
  3. Download your tax statements: the tax credit statement (Form 168, formerly 26AS) and the Annual Information Statement, and match them to your bank and broker statements.
  4. Pick the form: ITR-2 for rent, interest, capital gains and salary; ITR-3 if you have business or professional income in India.
  5. Enter Indian income only, choose “non-resident” as residential status, and show exempt income such as NRE interest in the exempt income schedule.
  6. Claim treaty rates where they help, for example on NRO interest or dividends, with a tax residency certificate and Form 41 (formerly Form 10F).
  7. Give a bank account for the refund. An NRO or NRE account linked to your PAN is simplest. Non-residents without an Indian account can give a foreign bank account in the return.
  8. Submit and e-verify within 30 days, or the return is treated as not filed.

Documents to keep ready

  • Passport with entry and exit stamps for the year, to prove your days in India.
  • TDS certificates (Form 16A), Form 168 and the Annual Information Statement.
  • NRO, NRE and FCNR bank statements and interest certificates.
  • Capital gains statements from brokers and fund houses; for property, the purchase and sale deeds and cost of improvements.
  • Rent agreement and the tenant’s TDS details, if you let out property.
  • Tax residency certificate from your country and Form 41, if you claim treaty benefits.

Common mistakes in NRI returns

  • Filing as “resident” by default. It brings foreign income and the foreign assets schedule into the return, and has led to FAST-DS emails for people who lived abroad.
  • Using ITR-1. It is not available to non-residents.
  • Not filing because TDS was deducted. Refunds on rent, NRO interest and property sales come only through a return.
  • Forgetting to e-verify within 30 days.
  • Refund to a closed or resident savings account. Use an active NRO or NRE account linked to PAN.

Missed a year?

  • For income of 2025-26, a belated return can be filed until 31 December 2026 with a late fee, and a filed return can be revised until 31 March 2027 (with a fee after 31 December).
  • An updated return can be filed up to 48 months later, with additional tax.

Frequently asked questions

Which ITR form should an NRI file?
ITR-2 for most NRIs with salary, rent, capital gains or other income from India, and ITR-3 if they have business or professional income in India. ITR-1 is not available to NRIs.
Is foreign income of an NRI taxable in India?
No. Income earned and received outside India is not taxed in India for a non-resident; only Indian income is taxed.
Do NRIs have to report foreign assets in their Indian return?
No. The foreign assets schedule applies only to residents who are ordinarily resident, not to NRIs or RNORs.
How late can an NRI file an Indian return?
A belated or revised return can be filed within 12 months after the end of the tax year, with a fee; an updated return can be filed up to 48 months later with additional tax.
How can an NRI e-verify an income tax return from abroad?
Through the net banking login of an Indian bank, a code sent via a pre-validated bank or demat account, Aadhaar OTP if the linked mobile works abroad, a digital signature, or by sending the signed ITR-V by post to the processing centre in Bengaluru. Verification must be done within 30 days of filing.
What is the last date for an NRI to file the return for FY 2025-26?
The due date was 31 July 2026. A belated return can be filed until 31 December 2026 with a late fee, and a filed return can be revised until 31 March 2027.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
