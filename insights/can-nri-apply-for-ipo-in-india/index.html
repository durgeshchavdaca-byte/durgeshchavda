Can NRIs apply for IPOs in India? Yes — here’s how

Jio Platforms’ IPO closed for bidding on 23 October 2026, with allotment due on 26 October and listing on 28 October. Here’s how NRIs apply in Indian IPOs, what happens after bidding closes, and the tax.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 28 October 2026·6 min read

Quick answers
  • Can NRIs apply for IPOs in India? Yes — through ASBA from an NRE or NRO account, holding the shares in an NRI demat account.
  • NRE keeps proceeds fully repatriable; NRO is limited to USD 1 million a year.
  • Always check the issue’s red herring prospectus for NRI eligibility and category.
  • Tax: 20% if sold within 12 months; 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh after — details.
Key takeaways
  • NRIs can apply in Indian IPOs via ASBA from an NRE or NRO account with an NRI demat account.
  • NRE keeps proceeds fully repatriable; NRO is limited to USD 1 million a year.
  • Jio Platforms' IPO closed on 23 October 2026; allotment is due on 26 October and listing on 28 October (tentative).

How NRIs apply

  • Apply through ASBA (net banking or your broker’s UPI/ASBA flow) from an NRE or NRO account.
  • Hold the shares in an NRI demat account linked to the same type of account (repatriable or non-repatriable).
  • A PIS permission is generally not needed for IPO applications; some brokers restrict US and Canadian residents — check before applying.
  • Always check the offer document (RHP) for whether NRIs are eligible in that issue and in which category.

NRE or NRO?

  • NRE (repatriable): invest with money from abroad; sale proceeds can go back out freely after tax.
  • NRO (non-repatriable): use Indian income; proceeds stay within the USD 1 million-a-year repatriation limit.
  • More: NRE vs NRO vs FCNR.

Tax on IPO gains

Sell within 12 months: 20% short-term capital gains. After 12 months: 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh. Brokers deduct TDS for NRIs; treaty relief and credits at home may apply. Details: tax on IPO listing gains.

  • Oversubscribed issues allot retail shares by lottery, so many applicants receive none — the blocked money is simply released.
  • Proceeds go back to the account you applied from: NRE (fully repatriable) or NRO (repatriation within the USD 1 million a year limit, with Forms 145/146 where needed).
  • File an Indian return if TDS was deducted on your gains and you want any excess back, or if the treaty with your country of residence gives a lower rate — see tax on IPO gains.

Frequently asked questions

How many times was the Jio Platforms IPO subscribed?
About 22.19 times overall when bidding closed on 23 October 2026, per NSE data at 7 pm — around 70.5 times in the qualified institutional portion, 14 times for non-institutional investors and 2.7 times for retail.
When is the Jio Platforms IPO allotment and listing?
As reported from the issue timetable: allotment on 26 October 2026, unblocking of funds and credit of shares on 27 October, and listing on BSE and NSE on 28 October 2026 (tentative). Check status on the registrar's or exchange's website.
Can NRIs apply for the Jio Platforms IPO?
NRIs can generally apply in Indian IPOs through ASBA from an NRE or NRO account; whether and in which category NRIs could bid in a specific issue is set out in its red herring prospectus.
Can NRIs apply for IPOs in India?
Yes. NRIs apply through ASBA from an NRE or NRO bank account and hold allotted shares in an NRI demat account; check the offer document for NRI eligibility in each issue.
Should NRIs use NRE or NRO for IPO investment?
Use NRE for money brought from abroad if you want proceeds to be freely repatriable; NRO for Indian income, with repatriation within the USD 1 million-a-year limit.
How are NRI IPO gains taxed?
Shares sold within 12 months: 20% short-term gains; after 12 months: 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year. TDS is deducted, and a return can recover any excess.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
