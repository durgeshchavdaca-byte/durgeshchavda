Residential status: NRI, RNOR or resident?

Your residential status decides what India taxes: only Indian income, or your worldwide income. It’s worked out fresh every tax year, mostly from days spent in India.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·6 min read
Confirm your status hello@durgeshchavda.com

The basic tests

You are resident in India for a tax year if either:

  • you are in India for 182 days or more in the year; or
  • you are in India for 60 days or more in the year and 365 days or more in the four preceding years.

Otherwise you are a non-resident. These tests carried over unchanged into the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Special rules for Indians working abroad or visiting

  • For an Indian citizen leaving India for employment abroad (or as ship’s crew), the 60-day test doesn’t apply — only 182 days counts.
  • For an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin visiting India, the same relief applies — unless their Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh. Then the 60 days becomes 120 days (with 365 days in the four preceding years).

Deemed residents

An Indian citizen with Indian income above ₹15 lakh who isn’t liable to tax in any other country because of domicile or residence is treated as resident in India — typically people living in countries with no personal income tax. A deemed resident is treated as RNOR.

RNOR: the returning NRI window

A resident is Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) if any one of these applies:

  • non-resident in 9 of the 10 preceding years; or
  • in India for 729 days or less in the 7 preceding years; or
  • resident only because of the 120-day rule; or
  • a deemed resident.

An RNOR is generally taxed only on Indian income (and income from a business controlled from India), so returning NRIs often have one or more years to plan the move of foreign assets.

Frequently asked questions

How many days can an NRI stay in India?
Generally fewer than 182 days a year keeps an Indian citizen working abroad non-resident. For NRIs visiting India whose Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh, staying 120 days or more (with 365 days in the previous four years) can make them resident.
What is RNOR status?
Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident — a resident who, for example, was non-resident in 9 of the previous 10 years or spent 729 days or less in India in the previous 7 years. RNORs are generally taxed only on Indian income.
What is a deemed resident?
An Indian citizen with Indian income above ₹15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any other country because of domicile or residence. They are treated as resident but not ordinarily resident.
Did the Income-tax Act 2025 change residential status rules?
No. The residential status tests, including the 120-day rule and deemed residency, were carried over unchanged from the 1961 Act.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
