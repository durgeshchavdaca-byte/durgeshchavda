Setting up a US subsidiary of an Indian company

Your Indian company is selling to US customers and needs a US entity to contract, hire and get paid. Here’s how to do it inside FEMA — and keep the tax authorities on both sides happy.

By CA Durgesh Chavda·Last reviewed 26 September 2026·8 min read
Plan your US subsidiary hello@durgeshchavda.com

Why set up a US subsidiary?

  • US enterprise customers prefer contracting with, and paying, a US entity.
  • Hiring US sales and customer-success staff on US payroll.
  • US payment rails, marketplaces and app stores.
  • Keeping the Indian company as the parent — which suits companies raising from Indian investors or planning an Indian listing.

ODI limits for Indian companies

Investment by an Indian company in a foreign entity is Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) under the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Rules, 2022.

  • Automatic route: total financial commitment up to 400% of the Indian company’s net worth as per its last audited balance sheet (not older than 18 months), subject to an overall USD 1 billion per financial year; beyond that, RBI approval is needed.
  • Financial commitment counts equity, and also loans, guarantees and other support given to the subsidiary — not just share capital.
  • Bona fide business: the subsidiary must carry on real business activity; certain activities (such as some financial services) have additional conditions.
  • Layers: a structure where the US subsidiary invests back into India resulting in more than two layers of subsidiaries is not allowed.

Step-by-step

  1. Board approval in the Indian company for the investment and any loans or guarantees, and shareholder approval where the Companies Act requires it.
  2. Form the US company — usually a Delaware C-Corp, with the Indian company as the sole or majority shareholder. See C-Corp vs LLC and choosing a registered agent.
  3. Obtain the EIN — EIN without an SSN or ITIN.
  4. Remit the investment through your AD bank, which reports it in Form FC and obtains a Unique Identification Number (UIN) for the ODI.
  5. Submit evidence of investment (share certificate) to the AD bank within the prescribed six months.
  6. Open the US bank account — bank account guide.
  7. Put intercompany agreements in place before money or services flow between the two companies.

Ongoing FEMA reporting

  • Annual Performance Report (APR) for the US subsidiary — due by 31 December every year.
  • Annual FLA return by 15 July, since the Indian company now holds an overseas investment.
  • Reporting of further investment, loans, guarantees, restructuring or disinvestment through the AD bank.
  • Repatriation of amounts due from the subsidiary — dividends, royalties, fees — within the timelines the rules set.

Transfer pricing

Transactions between the Indian parent and its US subsidiary are international transactions under Indian transfer-pricing rules — and are also scrutinised by the IRS. Typical set-ups:

  • The Indian company builds the product and the US company resells it, with the US entity earning a routine margin.
  • The US company provides sales and marketing support to the parent on a cost-plus basis.

Document the method, benchmark it, and file the accountant’s transfer-pricing report in India each year — Form 3CEB for years up to FY 2025-26, and Form 48 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 thereafter. Treaty rates on payments between the two: India–US DTAA. Full detail, including the 2026 safe harbour: India–US transfer pricing.

US tax and filings

  • A C-Corp subsidiary pays 21% federal corporate tax on its profits, plus state taxes where it operates.
  • Because it is foreign-owned, it files Form 5472 with its Form 1120 for transactions with the Indian parent. Form 5472 guide.
  • Dividends to the Indian parent face US withholding, reduced under the India–US tax treaty (generally 15% for a company holding at least 10% of voting stock).
  • Delaware annual report and franchise tax by 1 March.

Subsidiary or flip?

A subsidiary keeps India on top; a flip puts a US company on top. If US venture investors will lead your next round, they may want the flip. If not, a subsidiary is usually simpler, cheaper to maintain and easier to unwind. Help with either: US Delaware holdco structuring or US company registration.

Frequently asked questions

How much can an Indian company invest in its US subsidiary?
Under the automatic route of the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022, total financial commitment can be up to 400% of the Indian company's net worth as per its last audited balance sheet, subject to USD 1 billion in a financial year. Larger amounts need RBI approval.
Which form is used to report ODI in a US subsidiary?
The investment is reported to the RBI through the company's AD bank in Form FC, which generates a Unique Identification Number. An Annual Performance Report is then due by 31 December each year.
Does an Indian parent need transfer pricing documentation for its US subsidiary?
Yes. Transactions between the parent and subsidiary are international transactions under Indian transfer-pricing rules and need an arm's-length method, documentation and an annual accountant's report (Form 3CEB up to FY 2025-26; Form 48 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 thereafter).
Does the US subsidiary file Form 5472?
Yes. A US corporation that is at least 25% foreign-owned files Form 5472 with its Form 1120 to report transactions with its foreign related parties, such as the Indian parent.

Please note: this guide is general information based on the rules as they stood on the review date above. Regulations and RBI/CBDT procedures change often — please get advice on your specific facts before acting.
