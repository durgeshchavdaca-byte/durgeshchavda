SEBI AIF in brief

  • Category I covers venture capital, angel, SME, social impact and infrastructure funds; Category II is private equity and debt; Category III runs trading strategies. See AIF categories explained.
  • Apply online on SEBI’s intermediary portal. The team needs at least one NISM-certified person and one with a relevant professional qualification.
  • Since July 2026 a scheme can launch 10 working days after filing its placement memorandum through a merchant banker, and the scheme fee for the first scheme is waived.
  • Category I and II AIFs are tax pass-through for most income. See AIF taxation.

Step-by-step: SEBI AIF registration process. Running an angel network? See angel funds under SEBI’s new rules.

GIFT City in brief

  • Register a fund management entity with IFSCA — an Authorised FME (USD 75,000 net worth) can run venture capital schemes.
  • Venture capital and restricted schemes open as soon as IFSCA takes the placement memorandum on record.
  • IFSC tax holiday of 20 years out of 25 for the manager, and exemptions for non-resident investors.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a venture capital fund in India?
Choose between a SEBI-registered Category I AIF (venture capital sub-category) in rupees or a GIFT City venture capital scheme in dollars; set up the sponsor, manager and fund vehicle; register with SEBI or IFSCA; file the placement memorandum; and declare first close within 12 months.
What is the minimum corpus for an AIF in India?
₹20 crore per scheme for SEBI AIFs. Social impact funds need ₹5 crore. A GIFT City venture capital or restricted scheme needs at least USD 3 million.
How long does it take to launch a SEBI AIF?
After registration, a scheme can launch 10 working days after its placement memorandum is filed through a merchant banker, under the process introduced in July 2026. Registration time itself depends on SEBI's review.
Should I set up my fund in GIFT City or as a SEBI AIF?
If most investors are Indian residents investing in rupees into Indian companies, a SEBI AIF is usually simpler. If investors are global or NRIs, or the fund invests outside India, a GIFT City scheme is often a better fit.
