Research from public filings — India’s hidden advantage

Every Indian company, private or listed, files its financial statements and annual return with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. That means you can see a private competitor’s revenue, margins, debt, shareholders and directors — something rarely possible in the US or UK. We pull and analyse these filings, plus GST registration and import–export codes, to size competitors, vet distributors and shortlist acquisition targets. How to run basic checks yourself: verifying an Indian company.

Cost and tax model

  • Subsidiary: can opt for a 22% base corporate rate (about 25.17% with surcharge and cess).
  • Branch of a foreign company: taxed at 35% plus surcharge and cess.
  • Money back to the parent: dividends, service fees and royalties each carry Indian withholding, reduced by your country’s treaty — see repatriating profits.
  • GST: 18% on most services; exports of services can be zero-rated.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in an India market entry study?
Regulatory mapping (FDI route, caps, licences), the recommended entity, a location view, a three-year cost and tax model, and competitor or partner research from public filings, ending in a go / no-go recommendation.
Can you get financial information on private Indian companies?
Yes. Indian private companies file their financial statements and annual returns with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, and these can be viewed for a small government fee. We analyse them for competitor and partner research.
Do foreign companies need government approval to enter India?
Most sectors allow 100% foreign investment under the automatic route with no prior approval. Some sectors, and investors with more than 10% beneficial ownership from countries sharing a land border with India, need government approval.
How is a foreign company's Indian subsidiary taxed?
As an Indian company. It can opt for a 22% base rate — about 25.17% including surcharge and cess — in exchange for giving up most deductions.
