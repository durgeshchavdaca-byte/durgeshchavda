Your home-country rules — and what they mean for an Indian partner

  • Bribery Act 2010, section 7: a company is liable if an associated person bribes on its behalf, unless it had adequate procedures — proportionate due diligence on partners and intermediaries is one of them.
  • Failure to prevent fraud (Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023), in force since 1 September 2025: applies to large organisations meeting two of — turnover over £36m, assets over £18m, more than 250 staff. The defence is having reasonable procedures, which the company must prove.
  • Recent enforcement shows the risk often sits with JV partners and local “fixers”.

Frequently asked questions

Does the UK failure to prevent fraud offence apply to Indian partners?
It can: a large UK organisation can be liable for fraud by associated persons acting for it, including overseas partners, unless it had reasonable procedures — which include risk-based due diligence on those partners.
What are adequate procedures under the Bribery Act for India?
Proportionate, risk-based procedures including due diligence on Indian agents, distributors and JV partners — ownership, government links, payment arrangements and track record — plus contractual controls and monitoring.
When did the India–UK trade agreement take effect?
On 15 July 2026.
