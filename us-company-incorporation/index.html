US incorporation

US company registration from India — Delaware C-Corp or LLC, done right on both sides

Most US formation services stop at the certificate. The expensive mistakes happen afterwards — on the Indian side and in year-one US compliance. We handle both.

Why Indian founders incorporate in the US

  • US investors and accelerators — many invest only in a Delaware C-Corp on standard documents.
  • US customers — enterprise buyers often prefer contracting with a US entity and paying a US bank account.
  • Payments and payouts — US payment processors, marketplaces and app stores are simpler with a US company.
  • Hiring and equity — US-style stock options for US team members.
The part most founders miss: if you live in India, owning a US company is an overseas investment under FEMA. It has to be routed and reported correctly through your Indian bank, and it changes what you disclose in your Indian tax return. See FEMA, ODI & tax rules when Indians own a US company.

What’s included

US side

  • Entity and state choice — C-Corp vs LLC, Delaware vs Wyoming vs your operating state. Compare C-Corp and LLC.
  • Formation — Certificate of Incorporation / Formation, registered agent, bylaws or operating agreement, founder share issuance and board consents.
  • EIN — IRS employer identification number, including for founders without an SSN or ITIN.
  • Bank account support — document pack and guidance for opening a US business account remotely.
  • Tax setup — elections, bookkeeping structure and the federal filings your entity type needs, including Form 5472 for foreign-owned LLCs.
  • Annual compliance — Delaware annual report and franchise tax, federal returns and state registrations where you have operations.

India side

  • ODI structuring — whether founders invest personally under LRS or through an Indian company, within the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022.
  • Form FC and bank documentation — reporting the investment through your AD bank, share-certificate submission and the annual APR.
  • Income-tax reporting — Schedule FA disclosure, foreign tax credit and TCS on remittances.
  • Transfer pricing — if an Indian company provides services to (or owns) the US entity.
  • Parent–subsidiary set-up — for existing Indian companies, see US Delaware holdco structuring.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Indian resident register a company in the USA?
Yes. Non-US residents can form a US corporation or LLC in any state. If you live in India, investing in that company is an overseas investment under FEMA and must be made and reported through your Indian bank under the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022.
Which is better for Indian founders — Delaware C-Corp or LLC?
If you plan to raise venture capital or issue stock options, a Delaware C-Corp is usually the right choice. LLCs can suit simpler businesses, but create extra US filings for foreign owners and can be treated differently for Indian tax purposes.
Do I need to visit the US to incorporate?
No. Formation, EIN and in many cases bank account opening can be completed remotely.
How do I get an EIN without an SSN or ITIN?
Foreign founders without an SSN or ITIN apply for an EIN using IRS Form SS-4 — by phone on the IRS international line, by fax or by mail — rather than the online application.
Do US companies still need to file a BOI report with FinCEN?
No. Under FinCEN's final rule effective August 2026, entities formed in the United States are exempt from beneficial ownership (BOI) reporting. Only foreign companies registered to do business in a US state still report.
What ongoing compliance does a Delaware company have?
Delaware corporations file an annual report and pay franchise tax by 1 March; Delaware LLCs pay a $400 annual tax by 1 June (raised from $300 by Delaware HB 400 for 2026 onwards). Federal returns (Form 1120, or pro forma 1120 with Form 5472 for foreign-owned LLCs) are generally due 15 April.
