Why it matters

FEMA reporting sits at the intersection of your AD bank, the RBI’s FIRMS portal, your company secretary and your valuer. Delays usually come from small mismatches — a FIRC that doesn’t match the allotment, a valuation dated after the issue, or a missing KYC. Late filings can be regularised by paying a late-submission fee, but repeated or long delays may need compounding and will be flagged in future due diligence.

Frequently asked questions

What is the deadline for filing FC-GPR?
FC-GPR must be filed on the RBI's FIRMS portal within 30 days from the date of allotment of shares or other capital instruments to a person resident outside India.
Who files FC-TRS — the buyer or the seller?
Generally the resident party to the transfer (buyer or seller) is responsible for filing FC-TRS, within 60 days of the transfer or of receipt/remittance of funds, whichever is earlier.
What happens if an FEMA filing is delayed?
Delayed reporting can usually be regularised by paying a Late Submission Fee (LSF) calculated on the amount involved and the period of delay, within the time window the RBI allows. Beyond that, compounding may be required.
Do startups with foreign investors need to file the FLA return?
Yes. Indian companies that have received FDI and/or made overseas direct investment must file the annual Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) return by 15 July each year.
