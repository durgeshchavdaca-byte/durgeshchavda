The India side: overseas direct investment (ODI)

Any investment by an Indian resident into the shares of an unlisted foreign company is ODI under FEMA’s Overseas Investment Rules, 2022 — whether it’s USD 1 or USD 1 million.

Where the company is managed from

If the key management and commercial decisions of your foreign company are actually made in India, India can treat it as an Indian tax resident (“place of effective management”). Under the CBDT’s circular this applies to foreign companies with turnover above ₹50 crore, and treaty tie-breakers look at the same question. Plan board meetings, signatories and decision-making accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Indian resident set up a company abroad?
Yes. A resident individual can invest in an operating foreign company under the overseas direct investment rules within the USD 250,000 annual LRS limit, and an Indian company can invest up to 400% of its net worth under the automatic route, with reporting through an authorised dealer bank.
What filings are needed after investing in a foreign company?
Form FC through your bank when the investment is made, and an Annual Performance Report by 31 December every year; resident individuals also disclose the shares in Schedule FA of their income-tax return.
Can an individual's foreign company have subsidiaries?
If the resident individual has control of the foreign company, it cannot have subsidiaries or step-down subsidiaries under the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022. An Indian company has more flexibility.
Which country is best for an Indian startup's overseas company?
It depends on investors and customers: the US (Delaware) for US VC and SaaS customers, Singapore for Southeast Asian investors and an Asia base, the UAE for Gulf markets and capital, and the UK for UK and European customers.
