Due diligence in India

Due diligence in India for German and EU companies

European buyers sourcing from India face more supply-chain rules than ever — and an India–EU trade agreement is on the way. We check Indian suppliers and partners on the ground and document it for your compliance files.

  • LkSG duties still apply; reporting is suspended and an amendment is pending.
  • CSDDD applies from 26 July 2029 (Omnibus thresholds); the Forced Labour Regulation from 14 Dec 2027.

Your home-country rules — and what they mean for an Indian partner

  • Germany’s Supply Chain Act (LkSG): the due-diligence duties still apply; the reporting duty has been suspended in practice and an amendment is pending.
  • EU CSDDD after the 2026 Omnibus (Directive (EU) 2026/470): applies to EU companies with 5,000+ employees and over €1.5bn turnover, and non-EU companies with over €1.5bn EU turnover — from 26 July 2029. Their suppliers will be asked for evidence well before then.
  • EU Forced Labour Regulation: applies from 14 December 2027 — products made with forced labour can be banned from the EU market.
  • CBAM: the definitive phase started on 1 January 2026. EU importers of Indian steel and aluminium need verified emissions data; without it, default values raise costs (small importers of 50 tonnes a year or less are exempt).

Frequently asked questions

Do German companies still need supply-chain due diligence on Indian suppliers?
Yes. Under Germany's Supply Chain Act the due-diligence duties continue even though the reporting obligation has been suspended in practice and an amendment is pending.
When does the EU CSDDD apply?
After the 2026 Omnibus amendments, from 26 July 2029, for EU companies with 5,000+ employees and over €1.5bn turnover, and non-EU companies with over €1.5bn turnover in the EU.
How does CBAM affect Indian steel and aluminium suppliers?
Since 1 January 2026 EU importers pay for embedded emissions; they need verified emissions data from Indian suppliers, otherwise higher default values apply.
