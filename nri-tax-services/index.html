Selling property in India

When an NRI sells, the buyer must deduct TDS on the capital gain — on long-term gains at 12.5% plus surcharge and cess, which can take the effective rate to about 14.95%. From 1 October 2026, individual buyers no longer need a TAN to do this. A lower-TDS certificate, obtained before the sale, stops tax being over-deducted on the full sale price. Full guide: TDS on sale of property by an NRI.

Moving money out

NRIs can remit up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO balances and sale proceeds, with Form 145 and, for larger taxable amounts, a CA certificate in Form 146. See repatriating money from India.

Are you still an NRI?

Your status for the year depends on days in India — and, if your Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh, the 60-day threshold becomes 120 days. Returning NRIs can often be RNOR for a period, which keeps foreign income out of Indian tax. See residential status and RNOR.

Frequently asked questions

Do NRIs have to file an income tax return in India?
An NRI must file if Indian taxable income exceeds the basic exemption limit, and should file to claim a refund of excess TDS — common after a property sale or on NRO interest.
How much TDS is deducted when an NRI sells property in India?
On long-term capital gains, TDS is 12.5% plus surcharge (up to 15%) and 4% cess, an effective rate of up to about 14.95%. Short-term gains are taxed at slab rates, so TDS is typically 30% plus surcharge and cess. A lower-TDS certificate can reduce this.
How much money can an NRI repatriate from India?
Up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO balances, sale proceeds of assets and inheritance, with Form 145 and, where required, a CA certificate in Form 146.
Can you help without me travelling to India?
Yes. Returns, lower-TDS certificate applications, CA certificates and treaty-relief forms are handled online. Durgesh works with NRIs across the UAE, US, UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore and the Gulf.
